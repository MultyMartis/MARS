# Trust calibration checklist - MARS Forge

**Overlay only.** Run when governance credibility, confidence proportionality, uncertainty visibility, reliability disclosure, or trust survivability affects PASS, freeze, escalation, handoff, or report trust.

**Factory methodology:** [`../../projects/mars-website-factory/trust-calibration-governance.md`](../../projects/mars-website-factory/trust-calibration-governance.md).  
**Credibility model:** [`../../projects/mars-website-factory/governance-credibility-model.md`](../../projects/mars-website-factory/governance-credibility-model.md).  
**Trust drift taxonomy:** [`../../projects/mars-website-factory/trust-drift-taxonomy.md`](../../projects/mars-website-factory/trust-drift-taxonomy.md).

**Not:** runtime trust scoring, autonomous credibility assessment, universal trust law, or perfect reliability guarantee.

---

## 1. When to Run

Run this checklist when:

- PASS, PARTIAL, FAIL, freeze, delivery-readiness, or strong recommendation language may create trust beyond the evidence;
- QA confidence, reasoning visibility, escalation, governance economics, adaptive governance, meta-governance, or visual reconciliation affects perceived reliability;
- report polish, length, or professional tone may make partial evidence look stronger than it is;
- uncertainty, inferred validation, source-only checks, build-only checks, screenshot-only checks, or unverified states affect trust;
- institutional reliance on Website Factory, Forge, prior reports, or governance history may substitute for current evidence;
- failure, recovery, rollback, context compression, or handoff could damage credibility if proof boundaries are hidden.

---

## 2. Trust Calibration QA

- [ ] The trust claim is named: PASS, freeze, recommendation, escalation decision, delivery-readiness, or institutional reliance.
- [ ] The evidence basis is named: direct check, rendered inspection, source review, build output, screenshot comparison, inference, assumption, or unknown.
- [ ] Confidence language does not exceed evidence.
- [ ] Trust remains scoped to named files, sections, viewports, states, devices, sources, or checks.
- [ ] SAFE UNKNOWN is visible where evidence is missing.
- [ ] Report polish, maturity language, or process volume does not inflate perceived reliability.
- [ ] Trust claim can be traced to QA confidence, reasoning visibility, source lineage, escalation posture, and known unknowns where relevant.

---

## 3. Credibility-Survivability QA

- [ ] The report states what would make the trust claim fail or require revalidation.
- [ ] Failure, rollback, recovery, or future review would not reveal hidden overconfidence.
- [ ] Partial validation is not presented as broad reliability.
- [ ] Institutional trust remains reviewable, not assumed.
- [ ] Credibility can survive uncertainty because unknowns are disclosed.
- [ ] Continuity trust is readable for future operators after handoff, compression, or freeze review.

---

## 4. Confidence-Proportionality QA

- [ ] Strong confidence is backed by strong evidence.
- [ ] Inferred validation remains labeled as inferred.
- [ ] Source-only, build-only, rendered-only, screenshot-only, and partial checks are not promoted to universal confidence.
- [ ] Confidence is reduced or qualified when verification is local, partial, or unobserved.
- [ ] Escalation, HITL, or STOP decisions are not made more or less confident than authority permits.

---

## 5. Uncertainty-Visibility QA

- [ ] Unknowns are visible in the report, not buried behind summary confidence.
- [ ] Assumptions that affect trust are named.
- [ ] Missing source, missing rendered evidence, untested interactions, unverified states, unobserved breakpoints, or missing recovery evidence are disclosed.
- [ ] Uncertainty is paired with action: continue with disclosure, targeted QA, HITL recommended, HITL required, blocked, STOP, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
- [ ] Calibrated uncertainty is treated as credibility-preserving, not as a reporting weakness.

---

## 6. Explainable-Reliability QA

- [ ] The report explains why the conclusion is reliable within scope.
- [ ] Reliability is not implied by professional tone, checklist volume, or governance maturity.
- [ ] The reliability basis is understandable without private chat memory.
- [ ] Tradeoffs between confidence, cost, scope, escalation, and uncertainty are visible when material.
- [ ] A future operator can reconstruct the trust boundary.

---

## 7. Trust-Traceability QA

- [ ] Trust claim links to evidence, not only to a verdict.
- [ ] Trust claim links to reasoning rationale when a recommendation or prioritization is involved.
- [ ] Trust claim links to escalation posture when authority, ambiguity, contradiction, or approval affects continuation.
- [ ] Trust claim links to continuity or recovery evidence when future trust depends on handoff, freeze, rollback, or reconstruction.
- [ ] Trust claim links to drift taxonomy when false trust escalation, overconfidence, trust inflation, or perceived reliability drift is present.

---

## 8. Drift Checks

Flag `TRUST CALIBRATION FINDINGS` when any pattern appears:

- [ ] False trust escalation.
- [ ] Governance overconfidence.
- [ ] Credibility erosion.
- [ ] Confidence-performance mismatch.
- [ ] Trust inflation.
- [ ] Perceived reliability drift.
- [ ] Institutional overtrust.
- [ ] Blind governance reliance.
- [ ] Credibility collapse after failure.
- [ ] False-certainty trust.
- [ ] Performative confidence.
- [ ] Unverifiable trust signaling.
- [ ] Survivability-to-trust mismatch.
- [ ] "Professional tone therefore trustworthy."
- [ ] Trust theater.

---

## 9. REPORT Block

Use this block when material:

```text
TRUST CALIBRATION FINDINGS

- Trust claim: <PASS / freeze / recommendation / escalation / delivery-readiness / institutional reliance>
- Evidence basis: <direct / rendered / source / build / screenshot / inferred / assumed / unknown>
- Confidence adjustment: <none / narrowed / downgraded / escalated / SAFE UNKNOWN>
- Uncertainty visibility: <visible / partial / missing>
- Reliability explanation: <why trust is justified within scope>
- Drift pattern: <taxonomy name or none>
- Disposition: PASS | PARTIAL | FAIL | HITL RECOMMENDED | HITL REQUIRED | SAFE UNKNOWN | STOP
```

---

## 10. Not Claimed

- Runtime credibility scoring.
- Autonomous trust engines.
- Universal trust laws.
- Perfect reliability.
- Automatic failure prediction.
- Replacement for QA confidence, reasoning visibility, human escalation, or project-specific human authority.
