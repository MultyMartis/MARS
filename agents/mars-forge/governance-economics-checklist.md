# Governance Economics Checklist - MARS Forge

**Status:** Forge overlay QA checklist for Website Factory governance economics.  
**Methodology:** [governance-economics.md](../../projects/mars-website-factory/governance-economics.md), [operational-cost-awareness-model.md](../../projects/mars-website-factory/operational-cost-awareness-model.md), [governance-cost-drift-taxonomy.md](../../projects/mars-website-factory/governance-cost-drift-taxonomy.md).  
**Not:** autonomous governance optimization, runtime cost engine, universal economics law, automatic QA allocation, or perfect efficiency balancing.

Use this checklist when governance cost, review effort, QA depth, validation volume, process overhead, survivability cost, governance ROI, or operational sustainability affects the scope.

Record findings under **GOVERNANCE ECONOMICS FINDINGS**.

---

## 1. Cost Layer Selection

- [ ] Current governance cost is classified as one primary layer: lightweight-governance-cost, operational-standard-cost, elevated-review-cost, high-criticality-cost, continuity-preservation-cost, escalation-cost, or governance-sustainability.
- [ ] Selected cost layer is justified by scope, risk, uncertainty, evidence need, reversibility, blast radius, review burden, and continuity pressure.
- [ ] Lightweight cost is used when work is local, reversible, well-sourced, low-risk, and narrow.
- [ ] Operational-standard cost is used when the task needs normal Forge/foundation discipline without elevated uncertainty.
- [ ] Elevated-review cost is used only when specialist checks produce material evidence or risk reduction.
- [ ] High-criticality cost is tied to freeze, delivery, business meaning, source authority, accessibility trust, project identity, or release confidence.
- [ ] Continuity-preservation cost is justified by handoff, compression, long session, recovery, freeze restoration, or future resumability.
- [ ] Escalation cost is justified by authority, contradiction, approval, or human-owned decision boundaries.

---

## 2. Governance Efficiency QA

- [ ] Each invoked governance layer creates decision value, evidence value, risk reduction, or continuity value.
- [ ] Governance effort is not spent on duplicate findings that can be consolidated.
- [ ] Report blocks remain high-signal and scoped to active concerns.
- [ ] Full-depth governance is not used as a substitute for prioritization, confidence honesty, or escalation clarity.
- [ ] Governance value density remains readable to a future operator.
- [ ] Process does not become the primary artifact when the task requires execution, evidence, or freeze clarity.

---

## 3. Validation-Cost QA

- [ ] Validation depth is justified by material risk, evidence gap, uncertainty, or freeze/delivery consequence.
- [ ] Additional checks answer distinct questions rather than repeating the same evidence.
- [ ] QA depth is targeted, standard, focused elevated, full relevant, or escalation QA according to context.
- [ ] Expensive validation is not spent on low-impact, reversible, or already-covered concerns.
- [ ] PASS/PARTIAL/FAIL confidence does not depend on validation volume alone.
- [ ] Validation-cost explosion is named when checks expand without proportional confidence gain.

---

## 4. Review-Allocation QA

- [ ] Reviewer attention is allocated toward critical-path risk, source authority, accessibility trust, business meaning, freeze/delivery confidence, or continuity fragility when present.
- [ ] Cosmetic, informational, duplicated, or low-risk findings do not consume the same attention as critical or high-uncertainty issues.
- [ ] Risk weighting is used when cost allocation depends on severity or signal-to-noise.
- [ ] Escalation effort is reserved for material authority, contradiction, approval, or consequence.
- [ ] Review-cost imbalance is recorded when minor issues crowd higher-value review.

---

## 5. Sustainability-Balancing QA

- [ ] Governance depth remains sustainable across repeated sections, sessions, handoffs, and future operators.
- [ ] Survivability protection is strong enough for the risk without becoming unlimited ceremony.
- [ ] Continuity documentation improves resumability or recovery instead of creating archive burden.
- [ ] Governance cost does not make safe execution, freeze, or handoff disproportionately slow.
- [ ] The report names when depth was scaled down, targeted, deferred, or left unchanged for economic reasons.
- [ ] SAFE UNKNOWN is used when the survivability-to-cost balance cannot be proven.

---

## 6. Governance-ROI QA

- [ ] The operator can state what value the governance investment produced.
- [ ] Expensive governance leaves behind high-value evidence, clearer confidence, resolved authority, safer freeze, better handoff, or durable lessons.
- [ ] Diminishing returns are checked before adding more process.
- [ ] Governance cost is proportional to risk/value, not to anxiety, habit, or process aesthetics.
- [ ] Low-value governance is scaled down, deferred, grouped, or recorded as informational.
- [ ] Cost-aware reduction does not under-protect critical work.

---

## 7. Drift Checks

- [ ] No governance cost blindness.
- [ ] No process cost inflation.
- [ ] No QA resource drain.
- [ ] No operational overhead accumulation.
- [ ] No diminishing governance returns.
- [ ] No review-cost imbalance.
- [ ] No validation-cost explosion.
- [ ] No governance inefficiency.
- [ ] No survivability-to-cost mismatch.
- [ ] No governance resource waste.
- [ ] No expensive low-value governance.
- [ ] No process-weight escalation.
- [ ] No unsustainable governance complexity.

---

## 8. REPORT Format

Use this block when governance economics affects the scope:

```text
GOVERNANCE ECONOMICS FINDINGS

Cost layer: lightweight-governance-cost | operational-standard-cost | elevated-review-cost | high-criticality-cost | continuity-preservation-cost | escalation-cost | governance-sustainability
Cost driver: <risk / uncertainty / evidence need / review burden / continuity / escalation / sustainability>
Investment rationale: <why the governance cost is justified or why depth was reduced>
Governance efficiency: <decision value / evidence value / risk reduction / continuity value>
Validation-cost QA: <targeted / standard / focused elevated / full relevant / escalation QA / SAFE UNKNOWN>
Review allocation: <where reviewer attention was spent and why>
Survivability-to-cost balance: <why continuity protection is sustainable or what remains unknown>
Drift checked: <none | cost blindness | process inflation | QA resource drain | validation-cost explosion | ...>
Disposition: PASS | PARTIAL | FAIL | SAFE UNKNOWN | HITL REQUIRED | BLOCKED
```

---

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

- governance cost cannot be classified;
- validation depth cannot be justified;
- review allocation may be imbalanced;
- survivability protection may be too costly or too weak;
- scaling down may under-protect material risk;
- adding depth may create expensive low-value governance;
- governance ROI cannot be explained from available evidence.

Required statement:

- what cost/value evidence is missing;
- which cost layers are possible;
- what would justify keeping, increasing, reducing, or deferring depth;
- whether continuation is lightweight-safe, standard-safe, elevated-review-needed, HITL-needed, blocked, or deferred.

---

- No weakening of necessary governance where risk justifies depth.
