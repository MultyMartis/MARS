# Semantic Quality Rules v1

## Purpose

Defines quality rules for PPC keyword sets before campaign architecture. ORCA favors relevance, semantic cleanliness, and human review over automation or volume chasing.

## Core Quality Dimensions

- Intent purity - one cluster should serve one realistic user intent.
- Geo purity - regions, cities, districts, and service areas should not be mixed carelessly.
- Service purity - one cluster should not combine different services that require different ads or landings.
- Commercial purity - paid clusters should not absorb informational leakage unless explicitly approved.
- Audience purity - B2B and B2C terms should be separated when message, pricing, or landing differs.

## Contamination Risks

- Cluster contamination from informational terms.
- Duplicate intent across campaigns or ad groups.
- Broad match dangers from vague roots and short phrases.
- Mixed audience risks from business and private queries.
- Geo conflict between local and national wording.
- Service overlap that creates weak ad relevance.

## Operational Rules

- Keep low-confidence keywords out of core launch clusters.
- Separate urgent intent when operations can actually support urgency.
- Use negative keyword candidates to protect commercial clusters.
- Do not invent volume, priority, or performance.
- Mark SAFE UNKNOWN when source, meaning, or landing fit is unclear.

## QA Questions

- Can one ad message satisfy the full cluster?
- Does the landing page answer the cluster intent?
- Are any keywords likely to attract non-buyers?
- Is there hidden B2B/B2C conflict?
- Would broad matching this phrase create obvious waste?

## Boundary

These rules support human-supervised PPC quality. They do not publish keywords, manage match types, set bids, or optimize live campaigns.
