# ORCA Session Execution Guide v1

## Purpose

Answer one practical question: how does an operator actually run an ORCA live session without drowning in documentation?

This guide is for manual, human-reviewed PPC research. It is not automation, scraping, browser agent work, runtime orchestration, telemetry, dashboarding, or a new intelligence system.

## 1. Session Goal

The goal is useful PPC insight, not perfect documentation.

A good session should make the market clearer enough to support early decisions about SERP pressure, competitor positioning, CTA expectations, trust patterns, semantic risks, and landing requirements.

Speed matters. Realism matters. Usefulness matters more than completeness.

If a note does not improve a PPC decision or preserve important uncertainty, skip it.

## 2. Session Preparation

Prepare only what is needed to start.

- choose one niche;
- choose one city or tight region;
- open the relevant pilot package;
- open the query list;
- prepare a simple place for screenshots;
- decide mobile and desktop review order;
- limit the session before starting.

Do not prepare a full methodology stack. Do not open every ORCA document. Do not expand the pilot before observing the SERP.

## 3. Recommended Time Limits

Use time limits to prevent documentation drift.

- 10-15 min: mobile and desktop SERP review;
- 15-20 min: competitor review;
- 10 min: semantic risk notes;
- 10 min: strategic observations;
- 5 min: confidence and SAFE UNKNOWN;
- 5 min: operational review.

Stop earlier when diminishing returns appear.

If the operator is still collecting tiny details after the main patterns are clear, the session is already becoming too heavy.

## 4. Minimal Required Outputs

Absolutely matters:

- reviewed query list;
- screenshot references for important SERPs;
- visible ads, local pack, and aggregator pressure;
- top competitor CTA and trust patterns;
- obvious semantic risks;
- 3-7 short strategic observations;
- confidence limits;
- SAFE UNKNOWN.

Optional:

- full competitor page notes;
- detailed mobile / desktop comparison;
- local pack detail beyond obvious patterns;
- pricing examples beyond visible claims;
- extra screenshots for contradictions.

Can be skipped:

- complete UX audits;
- full semantic clustering;
- market theory;
- heuristic expansion;
- multi-region comparison;
- exhaustive competitor inventories;
- fields that do not affect thinking.

## 5. Live SERP Review Rules

Observe before interpreting.

Record only important patterns:

- ads and local pack presence;
- aggregators or directories;
- repeated competitor names;
- visible CTAs;
- pricing signals;
- urgency language;
- trust signals;
- obvious informational leakage.

Ignore noise. Do not document every result. Do not over-analyze snippets. Do not treat one SERP snapshot as stable market truth.

Use screenshots to anchor important observations, not to build a giant archive.

## 6. Competitor Review Rules

Open only a small set of visible competitors, usually 3-5.

Look for:

- CTA clarity;
- call, form, and WhatsApp prominence;
- trust proof;
- urgency and response promises;
- pricing visibility;
- mobile friction;
- local proof;
- differentiation.

Avoid giant UX audits. The goal is to understand commercial expectations, not redesign competitor websites.

## 7. Semantic Review Rules

Focus only on dangerous ambiguity.

Record:

- broad-intent risks;
- low-commercial-intent risks;
- equipment or service ambiguity;
- geo ambiguity;
- B2B / B2C confusion;
- aggregator or comparison intent;
- likely negative keyword candidates.

Do not build a full semantic core during the live session. Mark risks and move on.

## 8. Strategic Observation Rules

Keep observations short, conservative, evidence-aware, and revisable.

Good format:

- observed pattern;
- evidence reference;
- possible PPC implication;
- uncertainty.

Bad format:

- universal rule;
- confident claim without screenshot or repeated observation;
- theory about the whole market;
- vague "AI insight" language;
- recommendation that ignores evidence gaps.

## 9. Session Failure Signals

The session is failing when:

- notes keep growing but decisions are not clearer;
- the operator documents everything;
- the operator opens more documents than SERPs;
- abstraction replaces observation;
- one finding becomes a theory layer;
- confidence sounds stronger than evidence;
- screenshots become collection work instead of evidence support;
- operator fatigue appears;
- no actionable PPC insight is emerging.

When these appear, stop expanding and write SAFE UNKNOWN.

## 10. Session Success Signals

The session is working when:

- SERP structure is clearer;
- mobile and desktop differences are visible;
- trust patterns are easier to explain;
- CTA patterns are easier to compare;
- aggregator pressure is clearer;
- semantic risks are easier to avoid;
- campaign structure thinking improves;
- landing expectations become more realistic;
- uncertainty is explicit;
- the operator can summarize the useful output in plain language.

## 11. Stop Rules

Stop reviewing when:

- the main SERP patterns are already visible;
- additional competitors repeat the same signals;
- new notes are not changing PPC implications;
- evidence quality is too weak to support more conclusions;
- contradictions need another session, not more guessing;
- the operator is tired enough that note quality drops;
- the session is drifting into methodology, theory, or fake sophistication.

Low-value review should not be rescued by adding more fields.

If evidence is insufficient, write `SAFE UNKNOWN`, record the next verification step, and end the session.

## Final Operator Rule

ORCA live sessions should produce practical PPC intelligence quickly.

If the session feels designed to prove ORCA instead of learning from the market, it is too heavy.
