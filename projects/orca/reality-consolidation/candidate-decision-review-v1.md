# Candidate Decision Review v1

## Status

Decision review for usability only.

This file does not approve replacement of original ORCA templates or checklists.

## Classification

### KEEP AS ADDITIVE

Yes.

The candidate is useful as an additive operator shortcut for small, bounded PPC reviews where the issue is narrow and the action is reversible or human-approved.

### IMPROVE FIRST

Yes.

The candidate should be tightened before wider use. It is usable now, but the field count and some repeated wording may create avoidable operator fatigue.

### REJECT

No.

The candidate is not a pure abstraction layer. It produced a faster fictional review and preserved core safety signals.

### TOO BROAD

Partly.

The tradeoff section is broader than many small PPC checks need. It covers useful decision pressure, but it can overfit a tiny observation.

### TOO COMPLEX

Partly.

The structure is understandable, but too many fields look mandatory. Complexity is moderate, not fatal.

### TOO RISKY

No, if additive only.

Risk increases if the candidate is treated as a replacement for the original flow or used alone for high-impact decisions. As an additive shortcut, the safety posture is acceptable.

## Why This Classification

The candidate earns additive use because it keeps the operator in one file and makes the minimum safety checks visible:

- evidence strength
- confidence
- SAFE UNKNOWN
- weak evidence
- contradictions
- risk if wrong
- fallback action
- escalation triggers

That is enough for a small PPC issue.

The candidate does not earn replacement status because the original files give stronger separation for uncertainty, low evidence, escalation, and decision quality. That separation matters when the decision has spend, brand, legal, policy, or operational-capacity risk.

## Commercial Reality

Operators under time pressure are more likely to use one practical template than six separate files.

But operators under time pressure also skip long forms. The candidate must stay short enough to complete honestly.

## Safety Reality

Safety survived the fictional review.

The main reason: the candidate explicitly blocks weak-evidence escalation into strong claims.

Safety weakness: escalation triggers are compressed and less specific than the standalone escalation checklist.

## Decision

Keep the candidate as additive, but improve first before recommending routine use.

Do not replace the original templates/checklists.

Do not expand the methodology.

Do not promote the candidate as a universal review flow.
