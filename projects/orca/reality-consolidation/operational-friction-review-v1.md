# ORCA Operational Friction Review v1

## Status

Friction notes from the first fictional operational pilot simulation.

This is a usability review of the manual workflow, not a new methodology, governance layer, automation plan, or architecture expansion.

## Scenario Reviewed

- niche: emergency plumber
- city: Kazan
- scope: small PPC review for semantic cleanliness, landing fit, CTA clarity, trust signals, mobile friction, and escalation need
- primary output tested: fast operator decision quality

## What Slowed The Workflow

- Choosing among many available ORCA documents was slower than the actual PPC reasoning.
- Several layer names sounded relevant, but only a few changed the decision.
- Evidence discipline was useful, but the full evidence template would be too heavy if filled field-by-field.
- Campaign QA language was helpful only at the end; opening it early encouraged launch-readiness thinking too soon.
- Ad copy review was tempting to overdo before business claims were verified.

## What Felt Unnecessary

- Full competitor inventory.
- Detailed desktop review beyond major differences.
- Exhaustive search-term clustering.
- Any attempt to rate landing quality numerically.
- Broad theory about emergency-service markets.
- Writing long competitor UX notes.
- Turning every weak observation into a formal risk item.

## What Added Value

- `minimal-real-pilot-workflow-v1.md` gave the correct narrow path.
- `session-execution-guide-v1.md` made speed and stop-rules explicit.
- `fast-review/fast-review-workflow-v1.md` kept attention on decision-changing observations.
- `essential-signals/essential-signals-model-v1.md` helped separate signal from noise.
- `search-terms-review/search-term-cleanliness-rules-v1.md` quickly exposed urgent/generic/DIY/vacancy risks.
- `landing-match/landing-match-boundaries-v1.md` prevented fake conversion certainty.
- `ad-copy/ad-copy-boundaries-v1.md` prevented unsupported urgency, price, and guarantee claims.
- `campaign-qa-assembly/campaign-qa-boundaries-v1.md` helped mark the result as not launch-approved.

## What Created Fatigue

- Similar warnings repeated across layers: no automation, no fake certainty, human review required.
- Many directories contain useful-looking files, which increases selection load.
- Template fields can pull the operator into documentation performance instead of PPC judgment.
- Repeating SAFE UNKNOWN in every layer is safe but tiring when the session is already bounded.
- The operator had to actively resist expanding into landing audit, semantic core, and campaign launch QA.

## What Was Ignored

- Heuristic expansion.
- Large evidence normalization.
- Multi-region comparison.
- Full competitor review chain.
- Full semantic clustering.
- Advanced pattern libraries.
- Export or upload readiness.
- Any runtime, orchestration, telemetry, or automation framing.

Ignored because these did not improve the immediate PPC decision in a small pilot.

## What Repeated Itself

- Boundary reminders across landing, ad copy, campaign QA, and session guides.
- Human review requirements.
- SAFE UNKNOWN discipline.
- Anti-fake-certainty warnings.
- Operator fatigue / avoid-overdocumentation warnings.
- "Do not automate" statements.

The repetition protects against drift, but in a real operator session it should be compressed into one visible reminder.

## What Helped Decisions Fastest

1. Mobile-first emergency CTA check.
2. Semantic cleanliness check for urgent vs generic vs DIY/vacancy intent.
3. Landing match check: does the page support the query and phone-first behavior?
4. Trust-claim safety check: can the business prove 24/7, response time, price, guarantee?
5. Stop rule: once the core action was clear, stop adding notes.

## Friction Conclusion

The workflow works when treated as a short PPC judgment path.

It becomes heavy when the operator opens too many adjacent files, fills templates mechanically, or tries to prove ORCA instead of deciding the next PPC action.

Best operational rule from this pilot:

`Open fewer files. Capture fewer notes. Make the risky PPC decision clearer. Stop.`
