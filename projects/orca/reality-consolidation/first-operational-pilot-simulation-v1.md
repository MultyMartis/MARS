# ORCA First Operational Pilot Simulation v1

## Status

Fictional operational pilot simulation.

This is a manual PPC workflow test, not market research, automation, orchestration, governance expansion, or proof of live campaign performance.

## Pilot Frame

- niche: emergency plumber
- city: Kazan
- issue: urgent leak / pipe burst queries may mix with planned plumbing, DIY, vacancies, and generic repair intent
- review scope: 6 seed queries, mobile-first SERP scan, desktop contrast, 3 fictional visible competitors, one landing-fit pass
- search engine: generic search snapshot, fictional
- session target: decide whether the minimal ORCA path helps an operator catch obvious PPC risks quickly

- `аварийный сантехник казань`
- `сантехник срочно казань`
- `устранить течь трубы казань`
- `прорвало трубу казань`
- `сантехник на дом казань`
- `ремонт труб казань`

## Evidence Boundary

Evidence is fictional but commercially realistic. Treat all findings as a session simulation, not verified market truth.

Confidence cannot exceed `MEDIUM` because there are no live screenshots, CPC data, analytics, call recordings, or verified competitor pages.

## Short Flow Run

### 1. Mobile SERP Scan

Observed fictional pattern:

- emergency phrases produced call-heavy ads and local-pack pressure;
- generic `сантехник на дом` mixed urgent and planned-service offers;
- `ремонт труб` showed broader repair intent and possible planned work;
- directories/aggregators appeared around generic terms;
- visible trust signals were mostly response time, district coverage, phone prominence, and "без выходных" claims.

Operator implication:

- mobile review immediately matters because urgent plumbing demand is likely call-first;
- campaign should separate emergency leak terms from generic planned plumbing terms;
- ad and landing CTA must support immediate phone contact above the fold.

Confidence: `LOW-MEDIUM`.

### 2. Desktop Contrast

Observed fictional pattern:

- desktop made competitor comparisons and service lists easier to inspect;
- mobile was more decisive for CTA friction;
- desktop had more room for price and guarantee claims, but those claims were not enough to infer conversion strength.

Operator implication:

- desktop review is useful as a secondary check, but mobile carries higher operational weight for this niche.

Confidence: `LOW`.

### 3. Competitor Snapshot

Fictional competitors:

- Competitor A: strong phone CTA, "24/7", visible districts, weak pricing clarity.
- Competitor B: broad plumbing page, form prominent, phone present but not sticky, good reviews block.
- Competitor C: aggregator-style listing, many providers, comparison intent, weaker landing control for direct PPC.

Practical pattern:

- direct emergency competitors lean on phone, speed, local coverage, and trust proof;
- aggregator presence creates semantic risk on generic terms;
- weak pricing clarity is common, but unsupported price claims should not be copied.

Confidence: `LOW-MEDIUM`.

### 4. Semantic Cleanliness

High-risk terms:

- `сантехник работа казань` - vacancy intent, negative candidate.
- `как устранить течь трубы` - DIY informational intent, negative or separate content intent.
- `ремонт труб цена` - commercial but not necessarily emergency; keep separate from urgent ad group.
- `сантехник на дом` - broad commercial term; needs tight ad/landing match and negatives.
- `купить трубу казань` - materials intent, negative candidate.

Immediate PPC action:

- keep emergency leak / burst terms in a dedicated urgent ad group;
- separate generic home plumbing from emergency plumbing;
- review negatives for vacancies, DIY instructions, materials, and free advice intent;
- do not overblock price queries without checking commercial value.

Confidence: `MEDIUM` for semantic risk direction, `LOW` for exact keyword decisions.

### 5. Landing Fit

Fictional landing under review: emergency plumber page for Kazan.

Useful fit:

- city visible in hero;
- phone CTA present;
- emergency service named clearly;
- trust signals include reviews, technician photos, and service guarantee.

Friction:

- phone CTA not sticky on mobile;
- "from 500 rub" claim is visible but terms unclear;
- service list mixes emergency leak repair with installation of faucets, toilets, and planned work;
- no explicit district response boundary;
- WhatsApp exists but competes with urgent phone CTA.

Operator implication:

- improve mobile phone CTA clarity before scaling urgent terms;
- keep emergency promise honest unless dispatch coverage is verified;
- avoid sending pipe-burst queries to a broad "all plumbing services" page if the emergency block is weak.

Confidence: `LOW-MEDIUM`.

### 6. Ad Copy Fit

Safer draft direction:

- headline: `Аварийный сантехник в Казани`
- support: `Течь, прорыв трубы, срочный выезд`
- CTA: `Позвоните мастеру`
- trust: `Отзывы и гарантия на работы`

Avoid:

- guaranteed arrival time unless verified;
- fake scarcity;
- unsupported "lowest price" claim;
- broad plumbing copy for emergency-only queries.

Confidence: `LOW`.

### 7. Campaign QA Assembly Check

Fast QA decision:

- semantic cleanliness: needs urgent/generic split;
- landing alignment: acceptable only after mobile CTA and emergency block review;
- CTA consistency: phone-first should dominate emergency traffic;
- trust support: usable but should be verified;
- launch readiness: not enough evidence for launch approval.

Recommended action: `revise / collect more evidence`.

Escalation need:

- escalate to business owner before using response-time, 24/7, guarantee, or price claims;
- escalate if budget risk is material, because current evidence is a simulation.

## Strategic Observations

- Emergency plumbing PPC is likely mobile and phone-first, so mobile landing friction is a high-value review point.
- Semantic split between urgent leaks and planned plumbing is more useful than broad clustering.
- Aggregator/comparison intent appears risky on generic terms and should not be treated as direct emergency demand.
- Trust signals matter, but unsupported claims are more dangerous than empty pages.
- Price transparency helps only if the business can explain conditions clearly.
- The fastest useful decision was not "launch/no launch"; it was "separate urgent intent and fix mobile CTA before stronger spend."

## Operator Result

The minimal ORCA path was useful because it produced a bounded PPC decision quickly:

`Do not treat all plumbing queries as one group. Separate emergency leak intent, verify business claims, and fix mobile phone CTA before treating urgent traffic as launch-ready.`

The session should stop here. More detail would likely create fatigue before improving the decision.
