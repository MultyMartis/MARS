# ORCA Minimal Real Pilot Workflow v1

## Purpose

Answer the practical question: how does an operator run the first real ORCA pilot in the simplest possible way?

This document intentionally reduces complexity. It does not create a new methodology layer, governance structure, runtime system, automation, telemetry, browser agent, or autonomous PPC intelligence.

## Recommended First Pilot Types

Choose one local-service niche with visible search demand and practical PPC relevance:

- manipulator;
- cargo transportation;
- electrician;
- emergency plumbing;
- towing;
- local repair services.

Prefer one city or tight service region. Do not start with national markets, multi-region comparisons, complex B2B, or marketplaces.

## Minimal Required Workflow

### STEP 1 - Define Niche and Region

Record:

- service niche;
- target city or region;
- search engine;
- 5-10 seed queries;
- mobile and desktop as separate contexts.

### STEP 2 - Capture Mobile SERP

Human operator manually reviews the mobile SERP for the seed queries.

Record only:

- top visible competitors;
- ads and local pack presence;
- aggregator presence;
- obvious trust, price, urgency, and CTA signals;
- screenshot or manual review reference.

### STEP 3 - Capture Desktop SERP

Repeat the same review on desktop.

Do not over-analyze. The goal is to notice major differences from mobile.

### STEP 4 - Review Top Competitors

Open 3-5 visible competitors manually.

Record:

- primary offer;
- primary CTA;
- trust signals;
- pricing visibility;
- landing clarity;
- obvious weaknesses.

### STEP 5 - Record Trust and CTA Patterns

Write short observations:

- call-first, form-first, messenger-first, or mixed;
- phone visibility;
- local proof;
- reviews, licenses, guarantees, photos, or fleet/team signals;
- urgency language.

### STEP 6 - Record Semantic Risks

List only the most obvious semantic risks:

- mixed intent;
- information queries;
- aggregator or comparison intent;
- irrelevant geo modifiers;
- likely negative keywords;
- emergency versus planned-service mismatch.

### STEP 7 - Write Strategic Observations

Write 5-10 short observations that could affect campaign structure, ad copy, landing QA, or semantic cleanup.

Keep them bounded by region, device, and query. Do not turn them into universal heuristics.

### STEP 8 - Perform Confidence Review

Assign simple confidence:

- `LOW` - single snapshot, weak context, or contradiction exists.
- `MEDIUM` - repeated across several comparable queries.
- `HIGH` - repeated, current, traceable, and not contradicted in this small pilot.

Do not use `VERY_HIGH` in the first real pilot.

### STEP 9 - Record SAFE UNKNOWN

Record what is not known:

- conversion performance;
- CPC;
- lead quality;
- seasonality;
- competitor budget;
- personalization;
- whether findings repeat next week;
- whether findings transfer to another region.

### STEP 10 - Review Operational Usefulness

Answer:

- Did this workflow improve SERP understanding?
- Did it reveal semantic risks?
- Did it help campaign structure thinking?
- Did it improve landing or CTA review?
- Was the process fast enough to repeat?
- Which document or step felt unnecessary?

## What to Ignore During the First Pilot

Ignore unless the pilot clearly requires it:

- heuristic expansion;
- heuristic mapping complexity;
- market evolution tracking;
- broad contradiction mapping;
- large evidence normalization;
- giant review procedures;
- multi-region comparison;
- advanced pattern libraries;
- extensive agent role review;
- export package preparation;
- campaign upload readiness.

The first pilot is a reality test, not a full ORCA demonstration.

## Realism Rules

- Speed matters.
- Operational usefulness matters.
- Avoid over-documentation.
- Avoid abstraction addiction.
- Real findings matter more than elegant theory.
- Operator fatigue is a real risk.
- Contradictions should be recorded, not fully theorized.
- Evidence should be traceable, but not over-normalized.
- The pilot should produce practical PPC insight within one focused session.

## Failure Signals

ORCA is becoming too heavy when:

- the operator spends more time choosing documents than observing SERPs;
- the pilot requires more than one small subset of docs;
- fields are filled mechanically without changing decisions;
- observations become theory instead of PPC implications;
- confidence language sounds stronger than evidence;
- the workflow takes too long to repeat;
- the operator cannot explain the useful output in plain terms;
- contradictions trigger document sprawl instead of simple notes;
- the process feels designed to prove ORCA rather than learn from the market.

## Success Signals

ORCA is helping when:

- SERP structure is clearer after review;
- mobile and desktop differences are visible;
- competitor CTA and trust patterns are easier to compare;
- semantic risks are caught earlier;
- campaign structure ideas become more realistic;
- landing analysis has clearer evidence;
- PPC decision quality improves;
- uncertainty is explicit;
- the operator wants to repeat the workflow on another niche.

## Boundary

This is a minimal human-reviewed first-pilot workflow. It is not automation, scraping, browser agent work, telemetry, runtime orchestration, autonomous review, or autonomous PPC intelligence.
