# Aggregator Pressure Analysis v1

## Boundary

This document simulates aggregator pressure for a local-service SERP. It is not live SERP evidence and does not prove real dominance, CPC levels, or lead economics.

## Pressure Classes

- `local directories`: map listings, business cards, review directories.
- `service marketplaces`: multi-provider quote or booking platforms.
- `classifieds`: vehicle/service listings with phone exposure.
- `broad logistics aggregators`: cargo and transport marketplaces.

## Expected SERP Role

Aggregators may appear strongly for `аренда манипулятора`, `услуги манипулятора`, and price-modified queries. They can provide comparison convenience but may intercept leads before users reach independent providers.

## CPC Pressure Assumptions

Aggregator visibility can imply auction pressure, but CPC, bids, and budgets cannot be inferred from visible positions. Treat CPC pressure as a hypothesis only.

## Trust Distortion

- Aggregators may appear more trustworthy due to review volume, ranking lists, filters, or many listings.
- Individual providers may appear less mature if their landing pages lack photos, reviews, and pricing structure.
- Aggregator reviews may not reflect a specific provider's operational quality.

## Lead Interception Risk

Lead interception risk is plausible when aggregators offer one-click quote requests, multiple provider comparison, visible ratings, and fast phone access. This remains unvalidated without live SERP and user-path review.

## Defense Observations

- Local providers need clearer first-screen trust and direct contact.
- Ads should emphasize concrete equipment, fast quote, real availability, and local coverage.
- Landing pages should reduce the reason to return to a marketplace for comparison.

## SAFE UNKNOWN

Actual aggregator dominance, paid vs organic mix, lead quality, and user trust transfer are unknown. Re-observation is required before strategy recommendations.
