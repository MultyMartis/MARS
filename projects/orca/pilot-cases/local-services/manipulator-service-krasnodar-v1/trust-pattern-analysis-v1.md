# Trust Pattern Analysis v1

## Boundary

These trust patterns are simulated observations for local-service methodology testing. They are not validated market facts.

## Real-Phone Visibility

Phone visibility is likely a primary trust and conversion signal. For urgent manipulator orders, users may prefer a direct dispatcher number over a generic form.

## Equipment Photos

Real photos of the truck, crane, cargo examples, and work sites can reduce uncertainty. They help users understand whether the provider can handle pallets, building materials, cabins, containers, or equipment.

## Regional Trust Markers

- Krasnodar-specific service-area wording.
- Nearby districts and route coverage.
- Familiar local phone format.
- Local reviews or map-card presence.
- Mention of work with construction sites, warehouses, suppliers, or private customers.

## Response Speed Messaging

Response promises should be specific but cautious: `callback in 5-10 minutes` or `quick quote by photo` is more credible than broad guaranteed dispatch without conditions.

## Review Usage

Reviews may help when they reference punctuality, careful cargo handling, price transparency, and driver competence. Generic five-star claims are weaker than specific local job evidence.

## 24/7 Messaging

`24/7` can be strong for urgent logistics, but it creates a contradiction risk if calls are not actually answered outside business hours.

## Dispatch Trust Patterns

Trust improves when the landing page clarifies who coordinates the order, how vehicle availability is confirmed, and what information the user should prepare before calling.

## SAFE UNKNOWN

The true trust hierarchy may vary by B2B/B2C segment, season, cargo type, and urgency level. Human validation is required.
