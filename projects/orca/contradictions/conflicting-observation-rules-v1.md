# Conflicting Observation Rules v1

## Purpose

Defines how ORCA handles observations that point in different commercial directions. Conflicting observations protect the research process from unsupported abstraction and false certainty.

ORCA observes reality. ORCA does not define reality. A conflict is a signal to preserve context, not a reason to force agreement.

## Conflict Examples

- one region shows aggregator dominance while another does not;
- mobile SERP is local-pack heavy while desktop SERP is not;
- urgent CTA dominates in one season but disappears later;
- competitor landing pages emphasize price in one niche segment and trust in another;
- repeated snapshots show unstable ad density;
- client claims conflict with visible SERP evidence.

## Rules

- Record both sides of the conflict with source, timestamp, region, niche, and device.
- Identify whether the conflict is regional, seasonal, niche-specific, device-specific, source-related, or volatility-related.
- Use SAFE UNKNOWN when the cause is unclear.
- Cap confidence until the conflict is bounded or resolved.
- Split patterns instead of generalizing when evidence supports different contexts.
- Keep conflicting evidence attached to any downstream interpretation.
- Human review is required before using a conflicted finding strategically.

## Forbidden Handling

- deleting the weaker observation;
- hiding the conflict from summaries;
- converting one side into a rule without repeated evidence;
- treating a newer observation as automatically superior;
- treating AI-generated synthesis as conflict resolution.

## Boundary

These rules govern evidence interpretation. They are not a decision engine, runtime analytics, automated QA, or market intelligence automation.
