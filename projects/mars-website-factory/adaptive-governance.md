# MARS Website Factory - Adaptive Governance & Context-Sensitive Discipline

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory adaptive governance and human-supervised proportional-discipline methodology only.  
**Not:** autonomous governance adaptation AI, runtime policy engine, universal rigor law, automatic process selector, or perfect contextual scaling guarantee.

**Core principle:** frontend governance must preserve **context-sensitive rigor, proportional process depth, operational adaptability, risk-aware discipline, and project-fit governance**.  
It is not merely "apply governance," "run all QA," "use the full process," or "enforce identical workflow everywhere."

## 1. Positioning

Adaptive Governance & Context-Sensitive Discipline exists because Website Factory governance now includes prioritization, reasoning transparency, workflow discipline, provenance governance, temporal continuity, implementation survivability, escalation governance, QA confidence, governance minimalism, and visual/frontend intent layers.

That coverage is useful only when the governance depth fits the work.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Contextual proportionality, adaptive rigor, governance flexibility, operational fit, and context-aware discipline | Static process enforcement, maximum rigor everywhere, or identical workflow for every task |
| Human-supervised selection of lightweight, standard, elevated-risk, high-criticality, escalation-heavy, continuity-sensitive, or adaptive-review depth | Automated policy engines, autonomous context classification, or universal rigor formulas |
| Drift vocabulary for over-governance, under-governance, static rigidity, context-blind QA, and governance scaling collapse | Replacing specialist governance layers or eliminating human judgment |
| Forge reporting discipline for `ADAPTIVE GOVERNANCE FINDINGS` | Runtime governance adaptation, automatic QA-depth allocation, or perfect operational fit |

The governance question is not "did we apply enough process?"  
The governance question is: **does the rigor level match the scope, risk, uncertainty, reversibility, continuity pressure, and operational consequence of this work?**

---

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Adaptive governance** is the discipline of scaling governance depth to the actual operational context instead of treating all frontend work as if it carries the same risk.

**Context-sensitive discipline** is the practice of keeping process strict where consequence is high, light where risk is low, and explainable wherever the chosen depth affects confidence, escalation, or survivability.

This layer protects:

- **Contextual proportionality** - simple, reversible, low-risk work does not inherit full critical-path ceremony by default.
- **Adaptive rigor** - high-risk, authority-sensitive, source-ambiguous, or continuity-critical work receives stronger review.
- **Governance flexibility** - optional depth, escalation-only paths, and focused QA remain available without becoming universal mandates.
- **Operational fit** - the process is usable by real operators under the current project stage, evidence state, and time pressure.
- **Context-aware discipline** - discipline is not weakened; it is matched to risk, uncertainty, and consequence.
- **Adaptive survivability** - governance remains strong enough to protect critical work while light enough to survive actual execution.

A governance system may look highly disciplined, apply strong process, enforce many validations, and appear operationally mature while still applying the wrong rigor level, wasting operational resources, under-protecting critical work, or damaging execution survivability.

## 3. Required Vocabulary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Adaptive governance** | Human-supervised scaling of governance depth to task context, risk, uncertainty, and operational consequence. |
| **Context-sensitive discipline** | Strictness that changes by context while preserving honesty, authority boundaries, and evidence discipline. |
| **Proportional rigor** | Process depth matches scope, blast radius, reversibility, evidence gap, and criticality. |
| **Governance elasticity** | The ability to expand or contract review depth without abandoning governance principles. |
| **Adaptive QA depth** | QA scope selected by risk and evidence need, not by a fixed "run everything" reflex. |
| **Operational fit** | The governance path is appropriate for the current project, task, operator capacity, and delivery stage. |
| **Governance scaling** | The act of choosing lightweight, standard, elevated, critical, escalation-heavy, continuity-sensitive, or adaptive-review depth. |
| **Process proportionality** | Methodological weight remains justified by the value and risk it addresses. |
| **Context-aware escalation** | HITL depth follows authority, ambiguity, contradiction, and consequence instead of habit. |
| **Governance mismatch** | The selected governance depth does not fit the task's actual risk or operational context. |
| **Adaptive survivability** | The governance method can protect critical work without exhausting operators or collapsing into ritual. |
| **Governance rigidity** | A fixed process is applied regardless of context, risk, evidence, or consequence. |
| **Flexible discipline** | Governance remains disciplined while allowing depth to vary by justified context. |
| **Contextual risk alignment** | Rigor, QA, escalation, and reporting are aligned with the task's risk profile. |
| **Adaptive operational clarity** | The report makes the chosen governance depth and its justification readable to future operators. |

## 4. Core Rules

- **Rigor should match context.**
- **Governance depth requires justification.**
- **Not every task requires full process.**
- **Critical work requires stronger survivability.**
- **Operational fit matters.**
- **Governance scaling should remain explainable.**
- **Adaptability preserves usability.**
- **Proportional discipline improves survivability.**
- **QA depth should follow evidence need and consequence.**
- **Escalation depth should follow authority, ambiguity, contradiction, and risk.**
- **Lightweight governance is valid when the work is low-risk, reversible, local, and well-sourced.**
- **High-criticality governance is required when freeze, delivery, source authority, business meaning, accessibility trust, or continuity may be damaged.**

## 5. Adaptive Governance Method

Before choosing a governance path, identify:

| Question | Adaptive meaning |
|----------|------------------|
| What is the task scope? | Local edit, section slice, page-wide change, cross-page dependency, release/freeze decision, or recovery path. |
| What can break? | Visual intent, source authority, business meaning, accessibility, interaction/state behavior, workflow continuity, freeze, delivery confidence. |
| How reversible is it? | Easy local rollback, scoped correction, structural rewrite, HITL decision, or delivery blocker. |
| What evidence exists? | Direct source, active design pack, rendered evidence, build evidence, inferred source, ambiguous source, or missing evidence. |
| How much uncertainty exists? | None, low, moderate, high, contradictory, or authority-blocking. |
| Who owns the decision? | Existing source, project pack, operator prompt, governance rule, human decision, or SAFE UNKNOWN. |
| What governance depth is sufficient? | Lightweight, operational-standard, elevated-risk, high-criticality, escalation-heavy, continuity-sensitive, or adaptive-review. |
| What would be wasteful? | Full process, repeated QA, excessive reporting, escalation spam, or ritual depth without evidence value. |

**Rule:** the chosen rigor level should be stated when it materially affects confidence, escalation, freeze, or report scope. A short justification is better than hidden process selection.

## 6. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Identical governance everywhere** | Treats low-risk and high-risk work as operationally equal. |
| **Maximum rigor by default** | Wastes attention, slows execution, and creates checklist fatigue without matching risk. |
| **Context-insensitive QA** | Runs too much or too little QA because the task context is ignored. |
| **Static-process absolutism** | Turns useful methodology into rigid ceremony. |
| **Under-protection of critical work** | Applies lightweight process where source, freeze, delivery, accessibility, or business meaning needs stronger protection. |
| **Ritual depth inflation** | Adds process because it looks mature, not because it improves evidence or safety. |
| **Governance rigidity** | Refuses to adapt when context changes. |
| **Process scaling blindness** | Fails to explain why review depth increased or decreased. |
| **Fake operational maturity** | Large process volume hides poor contextual fit. |
| **"More process always better"** | Mistakes process weight for discipline, safety, or quality. |

Use [adaptive-drift-taxonomy.md](adaptive-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 8. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory adaptive governance lessons:

- Some local fixes need lightweight governance; forcing every visual, semantic, QA, escalation, workflow, context, and recovery layer at full depth can hide the next safe action.
- Some V2 freeze, source-version, mobile, CTA, proof, and business-intent questions require elevated or high-criticality review even when the visible UI looks stable.
- Missing mobile source, ambiguous screenshot authority, or conflicting project artifacts should increase discipline depth when consequence is material.
- Cosmetic spacing or icon concerns should not consume the same review depth as source authority, freeze integrity, or delivery-confidence risks.
- Governance minimalism and risk weighting are necessary but not sufficient; the system also needs explicit depth selection by context.
- A polished report can still be operationally wrong if it applies heavy process to low-risk work or light process to critical decisions.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Governance depth cannot be justified | Cannot prove whether lightweight, standard, elevated, critical, escalation-heavy, or continuity-sensitive process fits. |
| Task criticality is unclear | Cannot align rigor with consequence. |
| QA depth is disputed | Cannot tell whether full QA, focused QA, partial QA, or evidence disclosure is sufficient. |
| Escalation depth is unclear | Cannot decide whether continuation, disclosure, HITL recommendation, HITL requirement, or stop is appropriate. |
| Process weight exceeds visible value | Cannot prove operational fit or governance-to-value. |
| Criticality may be underestimated | Cannot safely use lightweight process where authority, freeze, accessibility, business, or delivery risks may exist. |
| Context changed during work | Cannot assume the originally selected governance depth still applies. |

**Action:** state the missing context, choose a provisional discipline layer, identify what would resolve depth selection, and disclose whether the current path is lightweight-safe, standard-safe, elevated-review-needed, HITL-needed, blocked, or deferred.
