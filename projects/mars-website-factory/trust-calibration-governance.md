# MARS Website Factory - Trust Calibration & Governance Credibility

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory trust-calibration discipline and human-supervised governance credibility methodology only.  
**Not:** autonomous trust engine, runtime credibility scoring, universal trust law, perfect reliability guarantee, or replacement for human judgment.

**Core principle:** frontend governance systems must preserve **calibrated trust, credibility survivability, confidence proportionality, explainable reliability, and sustainable governance trust**.  
They are not improved merely by "sounding confident," "looking professional," "producing long reports," or "appearing trustworthy."

## 1. Positioning

Trust Calibration & Governance Credibility exists because Website Factory governance now includes QA confidence, reasoning transparency, governance economics, adaptive governance, prioritization, meta-governance integrity, escalation governance, organizational memory, and workflow discipline.

That coverage is valuable only while trust remains proportional to evidence, reliability remains explainable, and credibility can survive failure, uncertainty, partial validation, and future review.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Calibrated trust, confidence proportionality, reliability transparency, trust traceability, credibility continuity, and sustainable governance trust | Runtime trust scoring, autonomous credibility engines, universal trust formulas, or perfect reliability guarantees |
| Human-supervised review of whether governance output deserves the trust it asks operators to place in it | Replacing QA, human escalation, reasoning visibility, or project authority |
| Drift vocabulary for false trust escalation, overconfidence, credibility erosion, trust inflation, and institutional overtrust | Redesigning Triumph or any other project |
| Forge reporting discipline for `TRUST CALIBRATION FINDINGS` | A claim that Website Factory can permanently prevent trust drift |

The governance question is not "does this sound trustworthy?"  
The governance question is: **does the visible evidence, uncertainty, reliability disclosure, and failure posture justify the trust being requested?**

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Trust calibration** is the discipline of keeping operator trust aligned with evidence, confidence, uncertainty, reliability history, and consequence.

**Governance credibility** is the degree to which a governance system remains believable, reviewable, and useful because it discloses what it knows, what it does not know, why its conclusions are reliable within scope, and where trust should stop.

This layer protects:

- **Calibrated trust** - trust does not exceed evidence, verification scope, reliability disclosure, or known uncertainty.
- **Governance credibility** - governance remains believable because it avoids fake certainty, inflated authority, and hidden gaps.
- **Confidence proportionality** - stronger confidence requires stronger evidence, clearer proof boundaries, and more visible uncertainty handling.
- **Reliability transparency** - operators can see what makes a conclusion reliable, partial, inferred, fragile, or unknown.
- **Trust survivability** - trust can survive errors because limits, uncertainty, escalation, and recovery posture were visible before failure.
- **Calibrated uncertainty** - uncertainty is shown as a credibility-preserving signal, not hidden as weakness.
- **Credibility continuity** - future operators can understand why trust was granted, withheld, escalated, or narrowed.
- **Explainable reliability** - reliability is tied to evidence and process boundaries rather than tone, polish, or documentation volume.
- **Trust traceability** - trust claims can be traced to evidence, QA confidence, reasoning visibility, source lineage, escalation posture, and governance boundaries.
- **Sustainable trust** - institutional confidence remains reviewable and does not harden into blind reliance.

A governance system may sound highly confident, produce polished reasoning, appear operationally mature, and provide extensive QA while still overstating reliability, inflating confidence, hiding uncertainty, and eroding trust after failure.

## 4. Core Rules

- **Trust should remain calibrated.**
- **Confidence requires evidence.**
- **Uncertainty visibility preserves credibility.**
- **Explainable reliability matters.**
- **Trust survivability matters.**
- **Credibility requires proportionality.**
- **Institutional trust should remain reviewable.**
- **Overconfidence destroys survivability.**
- **Professional tone is not proof of reliability.**
- **Long reports are not credibility.**
- **SAFE UNKNOWN is more credible than fake certainty.**
- **Trust claims must name their evidence boundary** when evidence is partial, inferred, source-only, build-only, screenshot-only, or not directly observed.

## 5. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Confidence inflation** | Language or report weight implies stronger reliability than evidence supports. |
| **Fake certainty** | Unknowns, assumptions, or inferred validation are presented as known fact. |
| **Performative trust** | Governance asks for belief through tone, maturity signals, or ceremony instead of evidence. |
| **Polished unreliability** | Reports look professional while reliability basis remains weak or hidden. |
| **Governance overconfidence** | Governance claims more certainty, coverage, or authority than it can support. |
| **Unverifiable credibility signaling** | Trustworthiness is asserted without traceable evidence or proof boundary. |
| **Institutional overtrust** | Operators rely on governance as a trusted institution without active review. |
| **Hidden uncertainty** | Known limits are omitted to preserve confidence aesthetics. |
| **Trust theater** | Process volume, checklists, or presentation substitutes for calibrated credibility. |
| **"Professional tone therefore trustworthy"** | Mistakes language polish for reliability evidence. |

Use [trust-drift-taxonomy.md](trust-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 7. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory trust-calibration lessons:

- A section can be visually polished, source-aligned in places, and heavily QA-described while still requiring calibrated uncertainty for unverified responsive, interaction, state, or accessibility behavior.
- Extensive governance creates trust pressure; without calibration, operators may over-believe long reports and under-see evidence gaps.
- Build success, screenshot similarity, or source review can support bounded confidence, but they cannot justify institutional trust in unobserved behavior.
- SAFE UNKNOWN preserves credibility when mobile source, interaction states, checkpoint evidence, or failure recovery proof is missing.
- Governance credibility depends on visible limits as much as visible strengths; hidden limits become trust debt.
- Failure is less damaging when prior reports already made proof boundaries, uncertainty, escalation, and reliability basis readable.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 8. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Trust basis is not traceable | Cannot prove why the system deserves the trust requested. |
| Confidence exceeds named evidence | Cannot treat the conclusion as credible without narrowing or disclosing scope. |
| Reliability basis is hidden | Cannot explain what makes the output reliable or fragile. |
| Uncertainty is suspected but not visible | Cannot know whether confidence language is masking gaps. |
| Institutional trust is assumed | Cannot rely on governance reputation without reviewable evidence. |
| Failure posture is unclear | Cannot tell whether trust would survive error, rollback, or contradiction. |
| Report polish is stronger than evidence | Cannot distinguish credibility from presentation quality. |
| Trust depends on prior memory or undocumented confidence | Cannot preserve credibility continuity across sessions. |

**Action:** state the missing trust evidence, narrow the confidence claim, expose uncertainty, name the trust boundary, and choose: calibrated PASS, PARTIAL trust, HITL recommended, HITL required, blocked, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
