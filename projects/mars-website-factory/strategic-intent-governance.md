# MARS Website Factory - Strategic Intent Preservation Governance

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory strategic governance methodology for human-supervised frontend production.  
**Not:** autonomous business AI, conversion-optimization engine, universal marketing truth, automatic strategic understanding, or runtime strategy enforcement.

**Core principle:** frontend implementation must preserve **business priorities, strategic messaging, conversion hierarchy, operational trust, proof structure, and stakeholder intent continuity**.

## 1. Positioning

Strategic Intent Preservation Governance sits above visual similarity, semantic correctness, responsive survivability, interaction quality, accessibility review, and QA confidence.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Business meaning, stakeholder priorities, conversion hierarchy, proof hierarchy, and operational seriousness | Universal marketing doctrine or automated conversion strategy |
| Human-supervised checks for strategy-to-implementation continuity | Autonomous interpretation of business goals |
| Drift vocabulary for business-message erosion, CTA dilution, proof collapse, and local optimization damage | Redesigning Triumph or any other project |
| Forge reporting discipline for `STRATEGIC INTENT FINDINGS` | A/B testing, analytics, telemetry, or optimization engines |

The governance question is not "does the frontend look polished?"  
The governance question is: **does the frontend still preserve the business reason this page exists?**

---

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Strategic intent** is the approved business meaning and priority structure that a frontend artifact must preserve while translating source material into UI.

It includes:

- the primary business objective;
- the intended conversion action and its priority;
- the stakeholder authority behind the message;
- the proof structure that earns trust;
- the seriousness level appropriate to the offer;
- the hierarchy between claim, proof, detail, risk, and action;
- the boundaries on local UI optimization.

A frontend may look modern, pass QA, feel responsive, and appear polished while still damaging business intent, weakening trust, eroding conversion structure, or collapsing operational seriousness.

## 3. Required Vocabulary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Strategic intent** | The approved business purpose, priority, and decision path the page must preserve. |
| **Business continuity** | The business meaning survives movement from source, strategy, copy, design, frontend, QA, and report. |
| **Conversion hierarchy** | The ordered relationship between primary CTA, secondary action, trust support, and deferral paths. |
| **Proof hierarchy** | The ordered relationship between strongest evidence, supporting proof, credibility cues, and decorative trust markers. |
| **CTA strategic role** | The business purpose of a CTA: commit, qualify, request, compare, learn, contact, or defer. |
| **Operational seriousness** | The interface reads as credible, grounded, and proportionate to the stakes of the offer. |
| **Business-semantic alignment** | Semantic structure and content labels support the actual business priority, not only local correctness. |
| **Strategic contamination** | Unapproved business tone, proof logic, CTA pressure, or stakeholder priority leaks into the build. |
| **Intent erosion** | Small local decisions gradually weaken the approved strategy. |
| **Local optimization drift** | A local UI improvement harms the global business message or conversion path. |
| **Strategic readability** | A human can read the page and understand what matters first, why it matters, and what action is expected. |
| **Stakeholder authority** | Named human/project/business authority that defines or approves intent. |
| **Conversion continuity** | CTA priority, role, placement, tone, and supporting proof remain consistent through implementation. |
| **Strategic escalation** | Ambiguous or contradictory business intent is surfaced to HITL instead of solved by taste. |
| **Business-trust preservation** | Visual, content, proof, behavior, and QA choices sustain trust rather than simulate it. |

## 4. Core Rules

- **Business priorities must survive implementation.** A correct component tree is not enough when priority order changes.
- **Conversion hierarchy matters.** Primary action, secondary action, proof, and support paths must not become equal-weight noise.
- **Proof should preserve authority.** Evidence must remain credible, placed, and weighted by business role.
- **CTA role must stay strategic.** A CTA is not a decorative button or animation target; it carries a business decision.
- **Local UI optimization can be harmful.** A prettier card, stronger hover, or louder badge can damage the global strategic path.
- **Operational seriousness is valuable.** Serious commercial interfaces may need restraint, not more engagement devices.
- **Stakeholder intent requires visibility.** When the source does not identify business priority or approval authority, record SAFE UNKNOWN.
- **Strategic drift must surface.** Do not hide conversion, proof, trust, or business-message changes inside "polish."
- **Implementation should preserve business meaning.** Design, code, interactions, and QA should support strategy, not replace it.

## 5. Business-Semantic Alignment

Semantic correctness answers whether the right labels, headings, entities, and landmarks exist. Strategic alignment asks whether those semantics still carry the intended business priority.

Failures include:

- a technically correct CTA label that now suggests a weaker business action;
- proof moved below a lower-priority detail section, reducing trust at the decision point;
- a hero that explains the offer but no longer identifies the strategic audience or urgency;
- equal card treatment that makes primary services, secondary services, and proof read as peers;
- stakeholder-approved messaging rewritten into generic "modern landing" language.

**Rule:** semantic QA and strategic intent QA are separate. Semantic PASS does not imply strategic PASS.

---

## 6. Conversion And Proof Hierarchy

Conversion hierarchy defines the path from business claim to decision.

| Element | Strategic expectation |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Primary CTA** | Carries the main business action and should remain visibly and behaviorally primary. |
| **Secondary CTA** | Supports learning, comparison, deferral, or lower-commitment action without competing as a peer. |
| **Proof near CTA** | Reduces uncertainty where the user is asked to act. |
| **Proof body** | Builds credibility in a paced sequence, not an undifferentiated trust wall. |
| **Operational detail** | Supports confidence, qualification, and seriousness without burying action. |
| **Decorative trust cue** | Must remain subordinate to real proof and never simulate authority. |

Proof hierarchy defines the relationship between evidence types. A certificate, case, review, metric, warranty, equipment detail, or process proof does not carry the same authority in every context. The implementation must preserve which proof is decisive, which is supportive, and which is merely contextual.

---

## 7. Strategic Readability

Strategic readability is present when a stakeholder can scan the frontend and answer:

- What is the main business promise?
- Who is the page for?
- What action is the page trying to earn?
- Which proof matters most?
- What objections or risks are being addressed?
- Which content is primary, supporting, or optional?
- Does the interface feel serious enough for the offer and audience?

Strategic readability can fail even when visual hierarchy is attractive. A page can have strong typography and polished spacing while the business story becomes generic, proof becomes wallpaper, CTAs multiply without role, or operational seriousness turns into fake premium styling.

---

## 8. Local Optimization Boundaries

Local optimization is allowed only when it preserves the global strategic path.

Examples of unsafe local improvements:

- making every CTA brighter to "increase engagement";
- adding motion to primary actions until trust declines;
- compressing proof into a single dense wall to save space;
- making a serious operational offer look like a generic SaaS campaign;
- equalizing all cards because the grid looks cleaner;
- replacing stakeholder-specific copy with smoother generic copy;
- adding decorative badges that compete with actual proof.

**Rule:** if a local improvement changes business priority, proof authority, CTA role, stakeholder message, or operational tone, it requires strategic review or HITL.

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift vocabulary:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is drift |
|--------------|-----------------|
| **Engagement-over-trust** | Optimizes motion, clicks, or excitement while weakening credibility. |
| **CTA spam** | Repeats or amplifies actions until hierarchy and trust erode. |
| **Proof overload** | Adds evidence volume until authority flattens and proof reads as decoration. |
| **Decorative seriousness** | Uses dark surfaces, heavy type, seals, or effects to simulate seriousness without proof. |
| **Fake premium business styling** | Adds luxury/SaaS polish that contradicts the operational stakes or source tone. |
| **Conversion inflation** | Promotes every action as urgent or primary. |
| **Stakeholder intent overwrite** | Replaces approved priorities with agent, template, or designer assumptions. |
| **Aesthetic-first redesign** | Makes the page look better while damaging business hierarchy. |
| **Strategic flattening** | Makes claim, proof, detail, CTA, and secondary content feel equally important. |
| **Business-message dilution** | Turns specific stakeholder meaning into generic marketing language. |

Use [strategic-drift-taxonomy.md](strategic-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 10. Forge Integration

When Forge is selected, strategic intent becomes a pre-freeze QA concern:

- Run [`strategic-intent-checklist.md`](../../agents/mars-forge/strategic-intent-checklist.md) when business priority, CTA role, proof hierarchy, stakeholder intent, operational trust, or conversion continuity affects implementation.
- Record **STRATEGIC INTENT FINDINGS** for strategic intent QA, conversion hierarchy QA, proof hierarchy QA, business-trust QA, stakeholder-intent QA, and strategic drift QA.
- Keep **STRATEGIC INTENT FINDINGS** separate from `DESIGN INTENT FINDINGS`, `CONTENT DENSITY FINDINGS`, `INTERACTION INTENT FINDINGS`, `QA CONFIDENCE FINDINGS`, and `SOURCE LINEAGE FINDINGS`, then summarize whether the frontend preserves the approved business path.
- Use [business-intent-continuity-model.md](business-intent-continuity-model.md) to identify the continuity layer at risk.
- Use [strategic-drift-taxonomy.md](strategic-drift-taxonomy.md) to name drift patterns.
- Use [temporal-evolution-governance.md](temporal-evolution-governance.md) when repeated local optimizations, modernization, freeze-state changes, or long-running edits may mutate strategic identity over time.
- Use [cross-project-transfer-governance.md](cross-project-transfer-governance.md) when strategic lessons, CTA/proof patterns, business assumptions, or visual trust logic are borrowed from another project; report `CROSS-PROJECT TRANSFER FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [design-intent-transfer-governance.md](design-intent-transfer-governance.md) when reconstruction fidelity affects business intent, conversion hierarchy, proof hierarchy, stakeholder meaning, or approximation transparency; report `RECONSTRUCTION FIDELITY FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [governance-prioritization.md](governance-prioritization.md) when strategic findings need severity weighting so business-critical CTA, proof, trust, or stakeholder risks are not diluted by lower-value observations; report `RISK WEIGHTING FINDINGS` separately.
- Escalate SAFE UNKNOWN when stakeholder authority, business priority, proof authority, CTA role, or strategic source intent cannot be proven.

This is human-supervised business continuity methodology. It does not claim automatic strategy understanding, conversion optimization, analytics, or autonomous business decision-making.

## 11. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory strategic lessons:

- A landing page can be visually improved while still weakening the strategic role of proof, CTA, or operational seriousness.
- Semantic rebuilds need business-priority preservation, not only correct section order and copy continuity.
- CTA clarity depends on strategic role, not only visual weight or repetition.
- Proof can collapse when reviews, specs, badges, and operational details become equal-weight trust noise.
- Industrial/commercial seriousness can be damaged by generic premium styling, SaaS defaults, or engagement-first interaction.
- Local section improvements can harm the global conversion path when they ignore stakeholder priority.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 12. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Strategic source intent is missing | Cannot identify the business objective from available artifacts. |
| Stakeholder authority is unclear | Cannot know which priority, CTA, proof, or tone is approved. |
| CTA role is ambiguous | Cannot decide whether action should commit, qualify, request, learn, compare, or defer. |
| Proof authority is unclear | Cannot rank evidence or decide what must remain near conversion moments. |
| Business and semantic signals conflict | A semantically correct structure may not preserve business priority. |
| Local optimization changes global intent | Need HITL to decide whether the change is improvement or drift. |
| Operational seriousness is not chartered | Cannot infer tone from taste, template, or generic industry assumptions. |
| Source lineage for strategy is weak | Cannot prove whether strategy came from active source, summary, prior output, or assumption. |

**Action:** state the unknown, name the needed resolver, and classify continuation as continue with disclosure, HITL recommended, HITL required, or stop.
