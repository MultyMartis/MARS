# MARS Website Factory — Content Overload Taxonomy

**Status:** **documented** — drift taxonomy for [Content Density Governance](content-density-governance.md).  
**Not:** automated detector, content quality score, SEO tool, copywriting engine, or universal density truth.

**Purpose:** Name recurring content-density drift patterns so Forge and Website Factory reports can distinguish useful high-density sections from uncontrolled overload.

---

## 1. Taxonomy Rule

High-density content is not automatically a failure.

A section fails this taxonomy only when density becomes uncontrolled: it erodes hierarchy, scanning rhythm, commercial seriousness, trust credibility, CTA visibility, or mobile readability.

---

## 2. Overload Patterns

| Pattern | Symptom | Risk |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Wall-of-text drift** | Long paragraphs or uninterrupted copy dominate the section. | Scanning rhythm collapses; important claims are buried. |
| **Endless-card drift** | Too many equal cards appear with similar weight and copy structure. | Narrative becomes inventory; user cannot prioritize. |
| **SEO stuffing feel** | Copy reads keyword-driven, repetitive, or explanatory beyond user need. | Commercial seriousness and trust degrade. |
| **Trust saturation** | Trust elements appear everywhere instead of at decision-support moments. | Trust becomes wallpaper rather than evidence. |
| **Proof spam** | Reviews, ratings, logos, numbers, badges, and claims repeat at high volume. | Proof credibility weakens through saturation. |
| **Feature-grid overload** | Feature cards multiply or carry too much microcopy. | Features flatten; core value becomes harder to scan. |
| **Dense-table collapse** | Table or comparison content is too dense for readable hierarchy, especially mobile. | Users cannot compare; detail becomes noise. |
| **Visual scanning exhaustion** | The eye has no clear rest or summary path across dense content. | Reader fatigue accumulates before CTA or proof resolution. |
| **Card-noise escalation** | Cards accumulate icons, badges, borders, links, and notes to solve unclear hierarchy. | Visual noise rises while clarity falls. |
| **Hierarchy flattening** | Heading, proof, detail, cards, and CTA all read at similar weight. | No element leads; section loses role. |
| **Microcopy overload** | Helper labels, caveats, notes, chips, and small print compete with primary meaning. | Decision support becomes distraction. |
| **CTA burial** | Primary action is surrounded or pushed down by dense content, proof, forms, or helper text. | Conversion path becomes hard to see or interpret. |
| **Operational seriousness collapse** | Content density makes the page feel like a spammy brochure, noisy dashboard, or fake authority wall. | The offer feels less credible despite more information. |

## 3. Drift Details

### 3.1 Wall-of-Text Drift

Occurs when copy volume is not shaped into scanable meaning.

Signals:

- paragraphs exceed the section's attention role;
- subheads do not summarize;
- proof and CTA are hidden after too much explanation;
- mobile turns the section into a scroll wall.

Governance response: summarize, group, or split only when copy authority allows; otherwise record **SAFE UNKNOWN**.

### 3.2 Endless-Card Drift

Occurs when the page keeps solving structure with more cards.

Signals:

- every message gets a card;
- cards have similar size, icon, heading, copy, and emphasis;
- section role becomes “grid” rather than proof, explanation, offer, or CTA;
- mobile becomes an endless stack.

Governance response: restore grouping, hierarchy, priority, or cadence; do not silently remove required content.

### 3.3 SEO Stuffing Feel

Occurs when copy reads as keyword coverage rather than user communication.

Signals:

- repeated service/location phrases;
- generic explanatory text that does not support a decision;
- page slows before proof or CTA;
- headings repeat instead of advancing meaning.

Governance response: flag as content authority issue; do not rewrite SEO strategy without approval.

### 3.4 Trust Saturation / Proof Spam

Occurs when trust evidence loses signal through repetition.

Signals:

- badges/logos/reviews appear in too many adjacent sections;
- proof blocks compete with the hero, offer, or CTA;
- proof lacks context or claim adjacency;
- ratings/certificates become decorative wallpaper.

Governance response: pace proof near the claim it validates; concentrate only where proof is the section's role.

### 3.5 Feature-Grid Overload

Occurs when feature/service grids become too numerous or too verbose.

Signals:

- grid count exceeds practical scanning;
- all cards have similar weight;
- card copy carries multiple ideas;
- icons and borders compensate for unclear priority.

Governance response: add hierarchy, grouping, or section split only with source authority.

### 3.6 Dense-Table Collapse

Occurs when comparison/spec/pricing tables fail readability.

Signals:

- too many columns or rows for the viewport;
- labels wrap until comparison becomes impossible;
- mobile table becomes cramped cards or horizontal fatigue;
- CTA and decision summary are separated from comparison insight.

Governance response: preserve required detail while adding summary, grouping, or responsive strategy if approved.

### 3.7 CTA Burial

Occurs when action is present but not perceptually available.

Signals:

- CTA is below too much proof or microcopy;
- primary and secondary actions blend into dense surroundings;
- helper text, badges, price notes, or forms compete with the action;
- mobile action appears after a long equal stack.

Governance response: protect CTA isolation and role without increasing pressure into CTA screaming.

### 3.8 Operational Seriousness Collapse

Occurs when more information makes the interface feel less trustworthy.

Signals:

- fake authority saturation;
- noisy dashboard feel;
- repeated badges and chips;
- every section tries to be proof, offer, and CTA at once;
- the page reads like aggressive persuasion instead of serious commercial communication.

Governance response: restore restraint, proof pacing, and clear section roles.

## 4. Anti-Patterns Index

Use these names in reports:

- wall-of-text drift;
- endless-card drift;
- SEO stuffing feel;
- trust saturation;
- proof spam;
- feature-grid overload;
- dense-table collapse;
- visual scanning exhaustion;
- card-noise escalation;
- hierarchy flattening;
- microcopy overload;
- CTA burial;
- operational seriousness collapse;
- trust-wall drift;
- proof saturation;
- dense-stack fatigue;
- density flattening.

---

## 5. Reporting Guidance

When a pattern appears, record:

- pattern name;
- affected section or sequence;
- whether density is required or accidental;
- effect on scanning rhythm;
- effect on proof/trust credibility;
- effect on CTA visibility;
- mobile amplification, if any;
- disposition: PASS / PARTIAL / FAIL / SAFE UNKNOWN.

---

## 6. SAFE UNKNOWN

Classify as **SAFE UNKNOWN** when the reviewer cannot tell whether the density is:

- required by approved copy;
- mandated by SEO strategy;
- part of a proof inventory requirement;
- intentionally high-pressure for decision support;
- inherited from old/archived content;
- accidental implementation or card-system drift.

Do not remove, rewrite, hide, or reorder content without authority.
