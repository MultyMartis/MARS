# MARS Website Factory - Self-Refinement Model

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory self-refinement model for human-supervised governance evolution only.  
**Not:** autonomous self-improvement, runtime mutation, automatic governance rewriting, universal methodology lifecycle, or perfect adaptability guarantee.

**Parent governance:** [governance-evolution-governance.md](governance-evolution-governance.md).  
**Drift taxonomy:** [evolutionary-drift-taxonomy.md](evolutionary-drift-taxonomy.md).  
**Forge checklist:** [`../../agents/mars-forge/governance-evolution-checklist.md`](../../agents/mars-forge/governance-evolution-checklist.md).

---

## 1. Purpose

The Self-Refinement Model defines how Website Factory governance may improve itself without becoming arbitrary, self-protective, historically unreadable, or endlessly additive.

It formalizes governance refinement as a human-supervised discipline:

- preserve what is stable and still justified;
- adapt what no longer fits current evidence;
- learn from repeated operational lessons;
- review governance assumptions before they fossilize;
- keep history readable when methodology changes;
- control the size, scope, and authority of each change;
- preserve continuity through every refinement.

This model does not give governance permission to mutate itself automatically. It gives operators a vocabulary for reviewing methodology change.

---

## 2. Layer Model

| Layer | Purpose | Review question |
|-------|---------|-----------------|
| **Stable-foundation layer** | Preserves core principles, honesty boundaries, human authority, and documentation-only scope. | What must remain stable for continuity and trust? |
| **Adaptive-refinement layer** | Allows scoped changes when context, risk, or evidence changes. | What needs adjustment, and why now? |
| **Lesson-driven evolution layer** | Converts repeated findings, failures, and reusable lessons into method updates. | What operational lesson justifies refinement? |
| **Governance-review layer** | Reviews whether current governance is stale, excessive, unclear, duplicated, or under-protective. | Is the current method still useful and proportionate? |
| **Historical-survivability layer** | Keeps older rationale, decisions, and report meaning readable after change. | Will future operators understand what was preserved, superseded, or retired? |
| **Controlled-change layer** | Bounds governance mutation through scope, authority, proportionality, and traceability. | Is the change controlled enough to avoid silent methodology drift? |
| **Continuity-preserving layer** | Ensures refinement does not break project history, governance memory, or cross-session readability. | What continuity must survive the refinement? |

---

## 3. Stable-Foundation Layer

The stable-foundation layer protects the parts of Website Factory governance that should not change casually:

- status honesty and SAFE UNKNOWN discipline;
- human-supervised execution boundaries;
- no runtime / autonomous enforcement claims unless implementation exists;
- source authority, lineage, and report traceability;
- HITL boundaries and no fake approval;
- documentation-only posture for governance methodology;
- continuity across project packs, Forge checklists, and Website Factory entry points.

Stable does not mean frozen forever. It means a change requires stronger evidence, explicit authority, and continuity-preserving rationale.

---

## 4. Adaptive-Refinement Layer

Adaptive refinement handles scoped method adjustment:

- clarify an ambiguous rule;
- narrow a rule that is too broad;
- expand a rule when repeated evidence shows under-protection;
- split overloaded governance into clearer concerns;
- merge duplicative rules when they produce the same evidence;
- convert mandatory depth into optional-depth or escalation-only treatment;
- retire or quarantine stale assumptions.

Adaptive refinement should be small enough to review and large enough to solve the actual governance problem.

---

## 5. Lesson-Driven Evolution Layer

Governance refinement should usually be lesson-driven, not novelty-driven.

Valid lesson sources include:

- repeated Forge findings;
- recurring drift patterns;
- Triumph V2 lessons with clear transfer boundaries;
- freeze-state or handoff failures;
- governance bloat or checklist fatigue evidence;
- source-lineage or context-survivability failures;
- human escalation patterns;
- organizational-memory discoveries.

A lesson becomes refinement candidate only when its source, scope, limitation, and reuse boundary are readable.

---

## 6. Governance-Review Layer

Governance review asks whether the method itself remains healthy:

- Does this layer still solve a real risk?
- Is its evidence distinct from adjacent layers?
- Has the method become too heavy for its value?
- Has it become too light for current consequence?
- Are old rules surviving without current rationale?
- Are new rules accumulating without pruning or classification?
- Can future operators understand how and when to apply the method?
- Does the method still preserve continuity, not merely process volume?

Governance review is not a demand to rewrite methodology. It is a disciplined check against stagnation, fossilization, and uncontrolled growth.

---

## 7. Historical-Survivability Layer

Historical survivability means governance changes should not make older documentation meaningless.

When refining governance, preserve:

- prior rule name or concept where still referenced;
- reason the old rule existed;
- what the new method preserves;
- what the new method supersedes;
- what remains valid for old reports;
- what should no longer be reused;
- whether the change is project-specific, Website Factory-wide, experimental, or SAFE UNKNOWN.

Historical survivability prevents refinement from becoming a continuity-breaking redesign.

---

## 8. Controlled-Change Layer

Controlled governance mutation should identify:

- **Change type:** preserve, clarify, refine, narrow, expand, split, merge, deprecate, quarantine, or experiment.
- **Evidence:** lesson, failure, drift pattern, operator friction, contradiction, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
- **Authority:** human decision, project governance, Website Factory doc, Forge checklist, or unresolved.
- **Scope:** current project, Website Factory, Forge overlay, reporting format, checklist item, or experimental note.
- **Impact:** affected reports, checklists, cross-links, lessons, and future operators.
- **Risk:** bloat, stagnation, continuity break, under-protection, over-protection, or mutation opacity.
- **Disposition:** accepted, partial, deferred, HITL required, blocked, or SAFE UNKNOWN.

The change record can be brief, but it should make future review possible.

---

## 9. Continuity-Preserving Layer

Continuity-preserving refinement keeps governance evolution readable:

- keep old and new meanings distinguishable;
- state what future reports should call the finding;
- preserve links from adjacent governance layers;
- avoid renaming concepts without a reason;
- avoid deleting old rationale from active meaning;
- record SAFE UNKNOWN where lineage or authority is missing;
- treat deprecation as a traceable state, not silent disappearance.

Continuity is not an excuse to keep every old rule. It is the discipline of making change survivable.

---

## 10. Refinement Dispositions

| Disposition | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| **Preserve** | Current governance remains valid and should not change now. |
| **Clarify** | Meaning is valid but wording, scope, or trigger needs clearer expression. |
| **Refine** | Method changes modestly while preserving the same governance purpose. |
| **Narrow** | Rule applies to fewer cases to improve proportionality. |
| **Expand** | Rule applies to additional cases because evidence shows under-protection. |
| **Split** | One overloaded rule becomes separate concerns. |
| **Merge** | Duplicative rules are combined to reduce bloat or confusion. |
| **Deprecate** | Rule is no longer active authority but remains historically readable. |
| **Quarantine** | Rule or method is unsafe to use until authority or evidence is resolved. |
| **Experiment** | Candidate method may be tested with explicit isolation and review. |
| **SAFE UNKNOWN** | Refinement cannot be classified from available evidence. |

---

## 11. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

- the reason for a governance change is missing;
- the old rule's historical purpose is unknown;
- the proposed refinement lacks authority;
- continuity impact is not reviewed;
- deprecation scope is unclear;
- experimental vs canonical status is ambiguous;
- the change may create hidden bloat or under-protection;
- future report compatibility is unknown.

**Action:** state what evidence is missing, keep the change bounded, and classify the next step as preserve, clarify, refine, deprecate, quarantine, experiment, HITL required, blocked, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
