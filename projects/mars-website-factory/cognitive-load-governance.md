# MARS Website Factory - Human Cognitive Load & Review Ergonomics Governance

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory review-ergonomics discipline and human-supervised cognitive-survivability methodology only.  
**Not:** cognitive-monitoring AI, runtime attention system, universal cognition law, automatic readability scoring, perfect readability guarantee, or permission to weaken necessary governance.

**Core principle:** frontend governance systems must preserve **human cognitive survivability, operational readability, sustainable review depth, attention clarity, reviewer sustainability, and signal-to-noise clarity**.  
They are not improved merely by "more detail," "longer reports," "more findings," "more terminology," "expanded QA coverage," or "maximum governance visibility."

## 1. Positioning

Human Cognitive Load & Review Ergonomics Governance exists because Website Factory governance now covers prioritization, trust calibration, reasoning transparency, adaptive governance, governance economics, meta-governance integrity, workflow discipline, organizational memory, QA confidence, and many frontend intent layers.

That coverage is useful only while humans can still read, review, prioritize, and act on it without cognitive collapse.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Cognitive survivability, review ergonomics, operational readability, reviewer sustainability, signal-to-noise clarity, attention allocation, and sustainable governance cognition | Runtime attention monitoring, cognitive profiling, universal cognition theory, automated readability scoring, or perfect report clarity |
| Human-supervised discipline for keeping reports and governance findings reviewable under finite attention | Reducing evidence honesty, hiding material risks, or deleting specialist governance |
| Drift vocabulary for reviewer fatigue, cognitive overload, unreadable reporting, governance exhaustion, review paralysis, and signal burial | Claiming that all reviewers have the same capacity or that readability can be guaranteed |
| Forge reporting discipline for `COGNITIVE LOAD FINDINGS` | Autonomous cognitive optimization, attention AI, or hidden report-pruning systems |

The governance question is not "how much detail can the system provide?"  
The governance question is: **can a real reviewer survive the report, see the signal, understand the next action, and sustain quality across repeated reviews?**

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Cognitive survivability** is the ability of a governance system to remain understandable, reviewable, and action-guiding under real human attention limits.

**Review ergonomics** is the discipline of shaping governance depth, report density, evidence placement, finding order, and escalation visibility so reviewers can process the output without fatigue, paralysis, or signal loss.

This layer protects:

- **Human cognitive survivability** - operators can apply governance without overload, exhaustion, or hidden priority collapse.
- **Operational readability** - reports communicate state, risk, evidence, and next action clearly enough for handoff and future review.
- **Reviewer sustainability** - review work can be repeated across sections, sessions, and projects without burning out the operator.
- **Attention clarity** - reviewer attention is directed toward material risk, uncertainty, escalation, freeze, and delivery consequence.
- **Signal-to-noise clarity** - useful governance signal remains visible above low-value findings, duplicate language, and report volume.
- **Governance readability** - rules and findings are human-readable, not merely comprehensive.
- **Cognitive load balancing** - review depth and report density match risk, consequence, uncertainty, and reviewer capacity.
- **Information-pressure survivability** - extensive evidence does not become unreadable pressure.
- **Sustainable governance cognition** - governance can stay sophisticated without becoming cognitively unusable.

A governance system may provide deep QA, contain extensive reporting, preserve strong traceability, and maintain sophisticated governance while still overwhelming reviewers, destroying readability, exhausting operators, and collapsing through cognitive weight.

## 4. Core Rules

- **Readability preserves survivability.**
- **Reviewer attention is finite.**
- **Review depth should remain proportional** to risk, uncertainty, consequence, and evidence need.
- **Signal clarity matters more than report volume.**
- **Governance must remain cognitively sustainable.**
- **Excessive density destroys usefulness** even when individual details are valid.
- **Sustainable cognition preserves quality.**
- **Governance readability matters** as an operational safety property.
- **Long reports are not automatically better reports.**
- **More findings are not automatically better QA.**
- **More terminology is not automatically better governance.**
- **SAFE UNKNOWN is required when review survivability, readability, or signal clarity cannot be established.**

## 5. Review-Ergonomics Questions

Before expanding report depth, invoking additional governance, or presenting findings, ask:

- Can a reviewer identify the highest-risk issue quickly?
- Does the report explain the next safe action before dense evidence?
- Is the amount of detail proportional to consequence and uncertainty?
- Are duplicate findings consolidated or cross-linked instead of repeated?
- Does added evidence improve decision quality, or only increase cognitive pressure?
- Are low-value observations grouped, demoted, or deferred?
- Would a future operator understand the report after fatigue, compression, or handoff?
- Does this report preserve attention for critical source, freeze, trust, accessibility, delivery, or escalation risks?
- Is the language clear enough to review, or has governance vocabulary become the barrier?
- Is cognitive load being reduced responsibly, or are material risks being hidden?

## 6. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Endless reports** | Report length grows until review cannot survive the output. |
| **Unreadable findings** | Findings contain detail but do not communicate consequence, priority, or action. |
| **Review overload** | Reviewer attention is consumed faster than useful judgment can be formed. |
| **Governance verbosity inflation** | More words are used to create authority rather than clarity. |
| **Signal burial** | Critical issues are hidden inside volume, categories, or repeated low-value details. |
| **Information-pressure escalation** | Evidence density rises without improving decision value. |
| **Cognitive exhaustion** | Operators become less able to review safely because governance is too heavy. |
| **Process readability collapse** | The governance process becomes technically complete but practically unreadable. |
| **Reviewer burnout** | Repeated review demands become unsustainable across project life. |
| **"More detail always better"** | Mistakes detail volume for quality, safety, confidence, or maturity. |

Use [cognitive-drift-taxonomy.md](cognitive-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 8. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory cognitive-load lessons:

- Deep visual, source, QA, escalation, workflow, context, and trust governance can become unreadable if every layer reports at equal density.
- Long V2 reports can look safer while making the next safe action harder to identify.
- Reviewers need critical-path summaries, proportional evidence, and visible dispositions before dense proof.
- Cognitive survivability is a delivery concern: exhausted operators are more likely to miss source authority, freeze, mobile, CTA, proof, or accessibility risks.
- Governance minimalism reduces bloat, prioritization orders risk, economics reviews cost, and cognitive-load governance protects the human review capacity across them.
- Sustainable Website Factory operation requires reports that survive repeated review, handoff, compression, and fatigue.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Report readability cannot be established | Cannot claim the output is reviewable by a future operator. |
| Finding volume may hide critical risk | Cannot prove signal-to-noise clarity. |
| Review depth may be disproportionate | Cannot tell whether density matches consequence and uncertainty. |
| Compression may hide material evidence | Cannot safely shorten without losing proof boundary, rationale, or escalation context. |
| Detail may create overload | Cannot prove added detail improves decision quality. |
| Reviewer sustainability is unclear | Cannot know whether repeated application will remain operationally viable. |
| Governance vocabulary blocks action | Cannot claim operational readability. |
| Cognitive load reduction may under-protect risk | Cannot scale down until criticality, evidence, and authority are clearer. |

**Action:** state the missing readability evidence, identify the highest-risk signal that must remain visible, classify the review layer, and choose: preserve detail, compress, group, defer, escalate, add summary, HITL required, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
