# MARS Website Factory - Governance Minimalism & Complexity Control

**Status:** **documented** - Website Factory governance discipline and human-supervised complexity-control methodology only.  
**Not:** autonomous simplification AI, universal minimalism law, automatic governance pruning, perfect balance guarantee, or removal of existing governance.

**Core principle:** governance must preserve **operational usefulness, proportional complexity, explainability, execution survivability, cognitive clarity, and usable discipline**.

## 1. Positioning

Governance Minimalism & Complexity Control exists because Website Factory governance now covers semantics, provenance, escalation, strategic continuity, temporal continuity, workflow discipline, context survivability, recovery, multi-agent coordination, implementation survivability, and many frontend QA dimensions.

That coverage is useful only while operators can still understand and apply it.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Governance proportionality, cognitive load, checklist fatigue, operational readability, process survivability, and governance-to-value ratio | Deleting governance, weakening quality gates, or replacing specialist layers |
| Human-supervised discipline for deciding when depth is essential, optional, escalation-only, or excessive | Universal minimalism doctrine across all projects |
| Drift vocabulary for governance bloat, process overload, ritualized QA, documentation inflation, and governance paralysis | Autonomous simplification, automatic checklist pruning, or hidden enforcement |
| Forge reporting discipline for `GOVERNANCE MINIMALISM FINDINGS` | A claim that perfect governance balance can be guaranteed |

The governance question is not "does a control exist?"  
The governance question is: **does this control preserve execution quality, clarity, and survivability at a complexity level justified by the risk?**

---

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Governance minimalism** is the discipline of keeping governance strong enough to prevent drift while light enough to remain usable by real operators.

It protects:

- **Operational clarity** - operators can identify what matters now without reading every possible layer.
- **Governance readability** - rules, checklists, and findings remain understandable.
- **Cognitive survivability** - the system can be applied without exhausting attention or hiding priorities.
- **Methodological proportionality** - control depth matches risk, scope, uncertainty, and project stage.
- **Usable discipline** - governance supports execution instead of replacing execution.
- **Governance survivability** - governance remains practical over long projects, handoffs, and future iterations.

A governance system may look sophisticated, appear comprehensive, contain many controls, and enforce many validations while still reducing execution quality, destroying usability, slowing iteration, and collapsing under its own complexity.

## 3. Required Vocabulary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Governance minimalism** | Keeping governance usable, proportionate, and value-producing rather than maximizing rule count. |
| **Proportional governance** | Matching governance depth to risk, ambiguity, blast radius, evidence gap, and project maturity. |
| **Complexity control** | Actively limiting methodological weight so governance remains readable and executable. |
| **Cognitive survivability** | Operators can apply the system without overload, fatigue, or hidden priority collapse. |
| **Methodological readability** | A governance method can be understood, explained, and resumed by a future operator. |
| **Governance inflation** | Governance expands through added rules, checklists, categories, and reports without equivalent operational value. |
| **Process fatigue** | Operators become less effective because the method demands too much ceremony relative to the task. |
| **Checklist overload** | Checklists multiply until they obscure priorities, create repetition, or encourage mechanical completion. |
| **Ritualized validation** | Validation is performed as ceremony rather than evidence-producing review. |
| **Governance entropy** | Rules, exceptions, reports, and layers accumulate until the system becomes harder to operate than the work itself. |
| **Operational simplicity** | The shortest sufficient governance path that still preserves quality, honesty, and escalation boundaries. |
| **Governance-to-value ratio** | The relationship between governance effort and the clarity, safety, quality, or survivability it creates. |
| **Methodological weight** | The cognitive, reporting, sequencing, and checklist burden added by a governance layer. |
| **Complexity escalation** | The point where a task legitimately needs deeper governance because risk, ambiguity, or dependency increases. |
| **Governance survivability** | Governance remains usable across long sessions, handoffs, future operators, and changing project pressure. |

## 4. Core Rules

- **Governance should stay usable.** A rule that operators cannot realistically apply becomes drift.
- **Complexity requires justification.** Added process should answer a real risk, not satisfy a desire for completeness.
- **Operational clarity matters.** If governance hides the next safe action, it has exceeded its useful weight.
- **Escalation is preferable to universal process.** Rare high-risk cases should trigger depth instead of forcing heavy process everywhere.
- **Optional depth is healthier than forced depth.** Deeper review can remain available without becoming mandatory for every small task.
- **Governance must preserve execution speed.** Slowness is acceptable for risk; slowness without value is bloat.
- **Readability matters.** Governance should be explainable to a future operator without private memory.
- **Systems should remain cognitively survivable.** A complete system that operators cannot hold in mind is not operationally complete.
- **Coverage is not the same as quality.** More categories can make findings less actionable.
- **SAFE UNKNOWN is better than performative completeness.** Unknowns should not trigger fake depth or fake closure.

## 5. Proportionality Questions

Before adding, invoking, or reporting a governance layer, ask:

- What risk does this governance step reduce?
- Is the risk present in this task, or only theoretically possible?
- Can the issue be handled by escalation-only logic instead of mandatory process?
- Does this checklist create new evidence, or repeat another checklist in different language?
- Is optional depth enough for this scope?
- Will a future operator understand the finding faster because this layer exists?
- Does this rule preserve execution survivability, or add methodological weight?
- Is the governance-to-value ratio still healthy?
- Does the task need a full layer, a short note, a deferral, or no additional governance action?

## 6. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Governance for its own sake** | Adds authority-looking process without operational value. |
| **Endless checklist expansion** | Treats every discovered risk as a permanent mandatory checklist item. |
| **Ritualized process theater** | Completion of ceremony replaces evidence, judgment, or useful findings. |
| **Mandatory depth everywhere** | Forces rare-case review onto low-risk scopes and slows execution without benefit. |
| **Methodology absolutism** | Treats a local governance method as universal truth. |
| **Over-documentation** | Creates so much documentation that source-of-truth readability declines. |
| **Governance density escalation** | Each layer adds more required reporting until operators cannot see priorities. |
| **Unusable operational complexity** | The method is technically complete but practically hard to operate. |
| **Process-over-outcome thinking** | The process becomes more important than quality, clarity, delivery, and honesty. |
| **"More rules = better system"** | Mistakes rule count for discipline, safety, or maturity. |

Use [governance-bloat-taxonomy.md](governance-bloat-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

When Forge is selected, governance minimalism becomes a pre-freeze, report, and handoff concern:

- Run [`governance-minimalism-checklist.md`](../../agents/mars-forge/governance-minimalism-checklist.md) when governance volume, checklist fatigue, process readability, finding sprawl, or methodology weight affects the scope.
- Record **GOVERNANCE MINIMALISM FINDINGS** for governance proportionality QA, cognitive-load QA, operational-readability QA, checklist-fatigue QA, process-survivability QA, and governance-to-value QA.
- Use [complexity-control-model.md](complexity-control-model.md) to classify essential governance, operational-value, lightweight validation, escalation-only, optional-depth, cognitive-load, and governance-survivability layers.
- Use [governance-bloat-taxonomy.md](governance-bloat-taxonomy.md) to name governance inflation, process paralysis, ritualized QA, checklist fatigue, documentation sprawl, methodological obesity, governance-over-execution, cognitive overload, excessive layering, performative governance, operational slowdown, survivability erosion, and governance collapse through weight.
- Keep **GOVERNANCE MINIMALISM FINDINGS** separate from `QA CONFIDENCE FINDINGS`, `HUMAN ESCALATION FINDINGS`, `WORKFLOW DISCIPLINE FINDINGS`, `CONTEXT SURVIVABILITY FINDINGS`, and `TEMPORAL EVOLUTION FINDINGS`, then summarize whether governance depth is proportional to the current task.
- Use [governance-prioritization.md](governance-prioritization.md) when the remaining findings still need severity weighting, escalation relevance review, signal-to-noise protection, or critical-path ordering; report `RISK WEIGHTING FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [adaptive-governance.md](adaptive-governance.md) when governance minimalism is not enough and the task needs explicit context-sensitive discipline selection, adaptive QA depth, or process-scaling review; report `ADAPTIVE GOVERNANCE FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [governance-economics.md](governance-economics.md) when governance weight creates operational cost, review-cost imbalance, validation-cost explosion, weak governance ROI, or survivability-to-cost mismatch; report `GOVERNANCE ECONOMICS FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [cognitive-load-governance.md](cognitive-load-governance.md) when complexity-control review specifically concerns report readability, reviewer fatigue, signal burial, review paralysis, or sustainable governance cognition; report `COGNITIVE LOAD FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [governance-compression-governance.md](governance-compression-governance.md) when complexity control must preserve deployable operational modes, compression integrity, mode transitions, or governance portability; report `GOVERNANCE COMPRESSION FINDINGS` separately.
- Use [governance-evolution-governance.md](governance-evolution-governance.md) when complexity-control review implies rule refinement, deprecation, merger, optional-depth conversion, or legacy-rule renewal; report `GOVERNANCE EVOLUTION FINDINGS` separately.

This is human-supervised complexity-control methodology. It does not create autonomous simplification, automatic governance pruning, runtime enforcement, or guaranteed balance.

## 8. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory governance minimalism lessons:

- A V2 rebuild can require many governance reads, but reporting every layer at full depth can hide the few findings that actually affect freeze.
- Dense governance helps only when findings remain prioritized, scoped, and readable.
- Visual, source, QA, escalation, workflow, temporal, and context checks can become checklist fatigue if invoked mechanically.
- Optional-depth and escalation-only logic are healthier than forcing every governance dimension into every section.
- Governance findings should improve operator confidence and next action clarity, not create ritual completion pressure.
- Long-lived projects need governance survivability: methods that remain usable after fatigue, handoff, compression, and repeated iteration.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Governance value is unclear | Cannot prove that added process improves quality, clarity, or survivability. |
| Checklist depth exceeds evidence needs | Cannot tell whether extra validation is useful or ritualized. |
| Findings are too numerous to prioritize | Cannot safely infer operational readiness from dense reporting alone. |
| A layer duplicates another layer's evidence | Cannot prove separate reporting adds value. |
| Operators cannot explain the governance path | Cannot claim cognitive survivability. |
| Governance slows work without named risk | Cannot justify methodological weight. |
| Escalation-only vs mandatory depth is unresolved | Cannot decide whether complexity is proportional. |
| Documentation has become the work | Cannot claim operational simplicity or execution survivability. |

**Action:** state the missing justification, identify the risk that would warrant deeper governance, and classify the path as essential, lightweight, escalation-only, optional-depth, defer, or remove-from-current-scope.
