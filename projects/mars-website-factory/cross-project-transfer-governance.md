## 1. Positioning

Cross-Project Knowledge Transfer Governance formalizes how Website Factory learns from one project without flattening another project into the same pattern, tone, visual language, strategy, or governance posture.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Transfer safety, compatibility review, transfer boundaries, contextual fit, and project-specific meaning | Universal pattern libraries, automatic reuse engines, or mandatory shared frontend standards |
| Human-supervised checks before reusing patterns, governance language, implementation approaches, visual treatments, or strategic assumptions | Autonomous compatibility detection or cross-project reasoning AI |
| Drift vocabulary for unsafe pattern reuse, false analogy drift, template contamination, and project-identity erosion | Redesigning Triumph or any other project |
| Forge reporting discipline for `CROSS-PROJECT TRANSFER FINDINGS` | Copying successful implementations as proof of fit |

The governance question is not "did this work before?"  
The governance question is: **does this transferred knowledge still fit this project's strategy, semantics, operational context, visual language, and governance authority?**

---

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Cross-project knowledge transfer** is the controlled reuse of lessons, patterns, vocabulary, checklists, implementation insights, or governance concepts from one project in another project.

**Transfer governance** is the discipline that decides what can travel, what must be adapted, what must remain project-specific, and what must be escalated before reuse.

It protects:

- **transfer safety** - reuse does not introduce hidden assumptions or wrong authority;
- **contextual compatibility** - the receiving project can actually support the pattern;
- **pattern integrity** - the pattern's purpose survives transfer instead of becoming cargo-cult structure;
- **strategic fit** - business intent, conversion hierarchy, proof logic, and operational seriousness remain project-specific;
- **project-specific meaning** - local vocabulary, brand tone, trust model, audience, and source authority are not flattened;
- **governance integrity** - rules are not copied as universal law when their authority was local.

A pattern successful in one project may weaken another project, damage strategic fit, distort trust, create semantic mismatch, or introduce governance drift.

## 3. Required Vocabulary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Transfer compatibility** | The receiving project can accept a pattern, lesson, or governance concept without strategic, semantic, visual, operational, or authority mismatch. |
| **Contextual fit** | The pattern fits the project's audience, offer, source state, design language, implementation stack, and governance maturity. |
| **Strategic inheritance** | A project adopts a strategic lesson from another project only after validating its business purpose and conversion logic. |
| **Semantic portability** | Meaning can travel without changing entity roles, CTA roles, proof authority, section purpose, or stakeholder intent. |
| **Operational mismatch** | The transferred pattern expects workflows, assets, stack behavior, evidence, or maintenance capacity the receiving project does not have. |
| **Transfer contamination** | Foreign assumptions, visual language, code habits, proof logic, or governance defaults leak into the receiving project. |
| **Template overreach** | A reusable template claims more authority than the receiving project grants it. |
| **Inherited assumptions** | Hidden beliefs from the source project are carried forward as if they were approved facts. |
| **Cross-project drift** | The receiving project moves away from its own strategy, semantics, or identity because transferred knowledge overpowers local context. |
| **Transfer survivability** | A future operator can still see what was transferred, why, what changed, and what remained project-specific. |
| **Governance portability** | A governance rule can be reused only after its scope, authority, and fit are validated for the receiving project. |
| **Pattern integrity** | A transferred pattern keeps its original reason, limits, and conditions rather than becoming visual or structural mimicry. |
| **Project-specific meaning** | Local strategic, semantic, visual, operational, and stakeholder context that must not be overwritten by reuse. |
| **Compatibility escalation** | HITL or explicit review is required because transfer fit cannot be proven from available artifacts. |
| **Transfer readability** | Transfer decisions are documented clearly enough for future operators to understand what traveled and what did not. |

## 4. Core Rules

- **Compatibility matters more than reuse.** A slower compatible transfer is safer than fast pattern copying.
- **Governance is not universally portable.** A rule created for one project may be local, temporary, or source-specific.
- **Semantics may be project-specific.** Similar sections can carry different CTA roles, proof authority, trust models, and audience meaning.
- **Strategic fit requires validation.** Business intent, conversion hierarchy, and operational seriousness do not transfer by analogy.
- **Inherited assumptions increase risk.** Prior success can hide context, constraints, and authority that no longer apply.
- **Transfer boundaries should stay visible.** Report what is reused, adapted, rejected, unknown, or escalated.
- **Project identity matters.** Reuse must not flatten brand language, offer seriousness, visual tone, or trust posture.
- **Reuse requires contextual awareness.** Pattern libraries, templates, checklists, and prior builds are inputs, not authorities.
- **Pattern integrity requires purpose.** Do not copy the shape of a solution without the conditions that made it valid.
- **SAFE UNKNOWN is valid.** If compatibility cannot be proven, disclose the gap instead of forcing reuse.

## 5. Transfer Boundary Discipline

Before transferring knowledge, classify it:

| Transfer candidate | Required boundary check |
|--------------------|-------------------------|
| **Implementation pattern** | Does the receiving stack, include graph, breakpoint model, token model, and maintenance capacity support it? |
| **Visual treatment** | Does the receiving project's design language, surface hierarchy, CTA tone, and trust model support it? |
| **Strategic lesson** | Does the business objective, audience, proof hierarchy, and conversion path match? |
| **Semantic structure** | Do entities, section roles, CTA roles, and source authority carry the same meaning? |
| **Governance rule** | Was the rule local, temporary, source-specific, or broadly valid for Website Factory methodology? |
| **Checklist item** | Is the receiving project exposed to the same risk, or does the check create template overreach? |
| **Template** | Which parts are reusable shell, and which parts require project-specific rewriting? |
| **Prior report finding** | Is the finding evidence, lesson, hypothesis, or source-specific conclusion? |

Transfer is unsafe when the boundary is invisible.

## 6. Strategic And Semantic Compatibility

Similar UI structure does not prove similar meaning. A hero, proof band, CTA block, service grid, comparison table, fleet card, review block, or lead form can have different roles in different projects.

Validate:

- whether the same pattern supports the same business objective;
- whether proof carries comparable authority;
- whether CTA pressure is appropriate for the offer;
- whether the audience reads the visual language similarly;
- whether local terminology and entities survive transfer;
- whether the receiving source actually authorizes the pattern;
- whether copied structure would change conversion or trust hierarchy.

**Rule:** a strategic or semantic mismatch blocks confident transfer until reviewed, adapted, or escalated.

## 7. Governance Portability

Governance can travel only with its scope and authority.

| Governance source | Portability posture |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| Website Factory core principle | May be reused as methodology, but still applied with project-specific evidence. |
| Triumph V2 lesson | Useful reference case; not a direct design or implementation command. |
| Forge checklist item | Applies when the risk is in scope; not every project needs every finding category every time. |
| Project-specific implementation pack rule | Does not govern another project unless explicitly promoted or adapted. |
| Prior agent report | Carries limited authority; requires provenance and compatibility review. |
| Template language | Must be rewritten where project meaning, source authority, or strategy differs. |

**Rule:** governance portability is a review outcome, not a default inheritance path.

## 8. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Blind pattern reuse** | Treats past success as current authority. |
| **Template absolutism** | Forces the receiving project into a reusable shell regardless of fit. |
| **Cross-project assumption inheritance** | Imports unstated source-project assumptions as local truth. |
| **Copied governance without validation** | Applies rules beyond their authority or risk scope. |
| **Aesthetic transfer without strategic fit** | Copies visual language that may weaken trust or business seriousness. |
| **Inappropriate frontend cloning** | Reuses implementation shape without stack, source, or maintenance compatibility. |
| **Project identity flattening** | Makes distinct projects read like the same brand, offer, or trust model. |
| **Transfer without compatibility review** | Reuse happens before strategic, semantic, operational, and governance fit are checked. |
| **Reusable-pattern dogmatism** | Treats reusable patterns as higher authority than project sources. |
| **Universal-solution thinking** | Assumes one frontend/governance/design solution should work everywhere. |

Use [transfer-drift-taxonomy.md](transfer-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 9. Forge Integration

When Forge is selected, cross-project transfer becomes a pre-freeze QA concern when prior projects, templates, governance lessons, visual treatments, code patterns, or report findings influence the current scope:

- Run [`cross-project-transfer-checklist.md`](../../agents/mars-forge/cross-project-transfer-checklist.md) when cross-project reuse, analogy, governance portability, inherited assumptions, or template transfer affects implementation.
- Record **CROSS-PROJECT TRANSFER FINDINGS** for transfer compatibility QA, semantic portability QA, strategic-fit QA, governance portability QA, incompatibility escalation QA, project-identity QA, and transfer traceability QA.
- Use [knowledge-transfer-compatibility-model.md](knowledge-transfer-compatibility-model.md) to classify transferable patterns, project-specific semantics, strategic compatibility, operational compatibility, governance portability, escalation/review, and incompatibility boundaries.
- Use [transfer-drift-taxonomy.md](transfer-drift-taxonomy.md) to name unsafe pattern reuse, false analogy drift, template contamination, copied-governance overreach, and project-identity erosion.
- Use [organizational-memory-governance.md](organizational-memory-governance.md) when transfer depends on reusable lessons, institutional memory, operational wisdom, lesson survivability, or rediscovery avoidance; report `ORGANIZATIONAL MEMORY FINDINGS` separately.
- Keep **CROSS-PROJECT TRANSFER FINDINGS** separate from `SOURCE LINEAGE FINDINGS`, `STRATEGIC INTENT FINDINGS`, `DESIGN INTENT FINDINGS`, `IMPLEMENTATION RELIABILITY FINDINGS`, and `CONTEXT SURVIVABILITY FINDINGS`, then summarize whether transfer is compatible, adapted, rejected, or escalated.
- Escalate **SAFE UNKNOWN** when transfer source, compatibility evidence, project fit, authority, or mismatch risk cannot be proven.

This is human-supervised knowledge portability methodology. It does not claim automatic compatibility detection, universal reusable systems, autonomous transfer AI, or reusable frontend law.

## 10. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory transfer lessons:

- A reference-case lesson can be valuable while still being unsafe to copy as design, code, or governance into another project.
- Industrial/commercial seriousness, CTA restraint, proof hierarchy, and operational trust are project-sensitive; they cannot be transferred by visual analogy alone.
- V1/V2 contamination showed that even nearby versions can leak wrong assumptions; cross-project transfer carries higher risk.
- A successful rebuild discipline can become template overreach if it ignores local source authority, brand language, or implementation capacity.
- Prior findings should travel as compatibility questions, not as automatic instructions.
- Transfer decisions need readable traceability so future operators know what was borrowed, adapted, rejected, or escalated.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 11. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Transfer source is unclear | Cannot identify what project, artifact, report, or rule is being reused. |
| Compatibility evidence is missing | Cannot prove the receiving project supports the pattern. |
| Strategic fit is unproven | Cannot know whether business intent, CTA role, or proof hierarchy matches. |
| Semantic portability is ambiguous | Cannot prove meaning survives transfer. |
| Governance authority is local | Cannot apply the rule elsewhere without promotion or adaptation. |
| Operational capacity differs | Cannot assume stack, workflow, assets, QA, or maintenance conditions match. |
| Project identity conflict is possible | Cannot prove the transferred visual or strategic language preserves local identity. |
| Template boundaries are invisible | Cannot tell which parts are reusable shell versus project-specific meaning. |

**Action:** state the transfer source, compatibility gap, likely risk, and disposition: compatible, adapt with disclosure, HITL recommended, HITL required, reject transfer, or blocked.
