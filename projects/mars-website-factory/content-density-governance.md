# MARS Website Factory — Content Density Governance

**Status:** **documented** — Website Factory preferred commercial content-density methodology for human-supervised frontend production.  
**Not:** automatic readability engine, density scoring system, SEO text maximizer, universal density truth, mandatory commercial style, or runtime enforcement.

**Core principle:** commercial frontend systems must control **information pressure**.  
Frontend quality is not only layout, spacing, rhythm, responsiveness, or visual continuity. It is also the discipline of deciding **how much meaning, proof, trust, and action pressure a reader can process at each page beat**.

## 1. Positioning

Content Density Governance sits between semantic completeness, visual reconciliation, cadence governance, responsive intent, and commercial trust design.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Information pressure, proof pacing, scanning rhythm, and overload risk | Exact copywriting length rules |
| Human-supervised density interpretation across sections and viewports | Automatic readability scoring |
| Website Factory preferred commercial readability philosophy | Universal minimalism or mandatory sparse style |
| Forge reporting vocabulary for content overload | AI content trimming, SEO optimization, or runtime adaptation |

The governance question is not “can we fit more content?”  
The governance question is: **does this amount of information strengthen commercial clarity without exhausting scanning, flattening hierarchy, burying CTA, or turning trust into wallpaper?**

---

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Information pressure** is the perceived cognitive and visual load created by text volume, proof volume, card count, grid density, CTA repetition, trust elements, badges, microcopy, media, and hierarchy competition in one section or sequence.

It increases when a section adds:

- many equal-weight cards;
- long paragraphs without scanning anchors;
- repeated trust/proof elements;
- dense specs, tables, prices, or feature grids;
- competing badges, icons, CTAs, and helper text;
- mobile stacks that preserve desktop volume without mobile recovery.

High pressure is not automatically bad. Some commercial sections must be dense: specs, proof, pricing, equipment lists, comparison tables, FAQ, operational constraints, and trust evidence can require density. The issue is **uncontrolled density**: density without sequencing, hierarchy, proof pacing, CTA survival, or breathing.

## 3. Required Vocabulary

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Information pressure** | The combined cognitive and visual load of content, proof, trust, CTA, and layout density. |
| **Scanning rhythm** | The reader's ability to move through headings, subheads, cards, proof, and CTA without losing orientation. |
| **Proof density** | Amount and concentration of reviews, numbers, badges, logos, cases, certificates, claims, and evidence. |
| **Density escalation** | Each next section adds more text/cards/proof because previous hierarchy did not decide what matters. |
| **Semantic overload** | Correct information becomes too concentrated to remain understandable or commercially persuasive. |
| **Trust-wall drift** | Trust elements accumulate into a wallpaper band rather than paced evidence. |
| **Card overload** | Too many cards or overfilled cards compete equally and flatten the reading path. |
| **Content flattening** | Different message roles become equal weight because everything receives similar visual/content treatment. |
| **Visual noise** | Badges, icons, borders, microcopy, proofs, and CTAs compete without a hierarchy reason. |
| **Readability exhaustion** | Reader fatigue from sustained dense text, grids, tables, or proof with no recovery beat. |
| **Dense-stack fatigue** | Mobile or vertical sequences become long equal-weight stacks that are technically readable but tiring. |
| **Hierarchy erosion** | Primary claim, supporting proof, detail, and CTA stop reading as distinct levels. |
| **Proof saturation** | Proof volume becomes less credible because every proof item shouts at once. |
| **CTA dilution by density** | CTA visibility and intent are weakened by surrounding cards, microcopy, proof, or badges. |
| **Information breathing** | Deliberate visual and narrative recovery that lets dense content be processed before the next pressure rise. |

---

## 4. Canonical Rules

- **Dense sections require breathing.** Before and after high-pressure content, provide cadence, grouping, or narrative reset so density is processed rather than endured.
- **Proof must be paced.** Logos, reviews, ratings, numbers, and certificates should support trust at the right beat; they must not become wallpaper.
- **Grids require scanning hierarchy.** Feature, service, proof, and equipment grids need title hierarchy, grouping, item priority, and readable rhythm.
- **CTA visibility must survive density.** Dense proof and helper text may support conversion, but must not bury the primary action or dilute CTA meaning.
- **Mobile density differs from desktop density.** A desktop grid can become an exhausting mobile stack unless mobile grouping, pacing, and recovery are reviewed separately.
- **Trust must not become wallpaper.** Repetition of badges, ratings, logos, and testimonials can lower seriousness when it reads as fake authority saturation.
- **Informational rhythm matters as much as spacing rhythm.** A layout can have correct gaps while still exhausting the reader through uncontrolled semantic load.
- **High-density sections are allowed.** They must have an explicit role, hierarchy, scan path, and recovery relationship to neighboring sections.

## 5. Density Governance Dimensions

Review content density through these dimensions:

| Dimension | Read for |
|-----------|----------|
| **Section pressure** | Is the section low, medium, or high pressure by role? |
| **Scanning ladder** | Can the reader quickly identify primary claim, proof, detail, and next action? |
| **Proof pacing** | Does evidence appear when it helps trust, not as continuous saturation? |
| **Card readability** | Are cards concise, grouped, and varied enough to avoid equal-card noise? |
| **CTA survival** | Does primary conversion remain visible and semantically clear? |
| **Trust seriousness** | Do badges, reviews, logos, and metrics increase credibility or become decorative authority spam? |
| **Mobile recovery** | Does a narrow viewport include breathing, grouping, and reset points? |
| **Sequence pressure** | Do adjacent sections create a controlled pressure arc or a dense-stack collapse? |

## 6. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift vocabulary:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is drift |
|--------------|-----------------|
| **Stuffing content everywhere** | Treats available space as an obligation rather than a communication decision. |
| **Endless grids** | Converts narrative into equal-weight inventory. |
| **Giant unreadable cards** | Forces every card to carry heading, text, proof, icon, and CTA without hierarchy. |
| **Proof spam** | Repeats reviews, ratings, logos, numbers, and badges until credibility weakens. |
| **Noisy dashboards** | Imports app-like panels, chips, tables, icons, and micro labels into commercial narrative. |
| **SEO-text wall** | Adds keyword volume that reads as stuffing instead of helpful explanation. |
| **Fake authority saturation** | Uses certificates, badges, seals, and proof markers as decoration rather than evidence. |
| **Endless trust badges** | Turns trust into wallpaper and lowers commercial seriousness. |
| **Density flattening** | Makes all content equally dense until hierarchy disappears. |
| **Microcopy overload** | Helper text, notes, labels, and caveats compete with main meaning. |
| **“More text = more value”** | Confuses information volume with persuasion, clarity, or trust. |

## 7. Forge / QA Expectations

When Forge is selected, content density is reviewed before freeze:

- Run [`content-density-checklist.md`](../../agents/mars-forge/content-density-checklist.md) alongside visual reconciliation, composition awareness, design intent, cadence, rhythm, and responsive intent QA when density, proof, trust, grids, cards, or verbose sections are in scope.
- Record **CONTENT DENSITY FINDINGS** when information pressure, scanning rhythm, proof pacing, trust density, card overload, CTA dilution, or overload taxonomy appears.
- Record `STRATEGIC INTENT FINDINGS` when density choices affect business priority, conversion hierarchy, proof hierarchy, operational trust, or stakeholder intent continuity.
- Classify failures using [content-overload-taxonomy.md](content-overload-taxonomy.md).
- Treat findings as human-supervised governance, not automated scoring.
- Escalate **SAFE UNKNOWN** when approved source, copy authority, SEO mandate, or HITL decision does not resolve density intent.

## 8. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory density lessons:

- A section can be semantically correct and visually polished while still overloading cards, trust rows, or proof grids.
- Dense service, equipment, price, proof, and review content needs pacing instead of maximum compression.
- Trust and proof can drift from credibility into wallpaper when every section tries to prove everything.
- Mobile stacks amplify density; desktop-acceptable proof/card volume can become narrow-viewport fatigue.
- Commercial seriousness degrades when every block carries equal text, equal proof, equal badge pressure, and equal CTA noise.

These are Website Factory lessons, not Triumph-only style rules.

---

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Missing copy authority | Cannot know whether dense text is required content, SEO draft, or accidental stuffing. |
| Conflicting proof requirements | Reviews, ratings, cases, or badges appear in multiple sources with no pacing decision. |
| Unclear commercial tone | Cannot decide whether high-density technical proof or restrained summary best fits the offer. |
| Missing mobile source | Mobile density sequencing and recovery cannot be chartered beyond readability checks. |
| SEO requirements not approved | Cannot distinguish useful explanation from SEO stuffing feel. |
| Trust role unclear | Cannot decide whether proof should be concentrated, distributed, summarized, or reduced. |
| Existing code overfilled but source is ambiguous | Cannot trim, split, or reorder content without authority. |

**Action:** document the resolver: approved copy deck, SEO brief, implementation-pack note, proof inventory, section-source matrix, responsive rule, or HITL decision.
