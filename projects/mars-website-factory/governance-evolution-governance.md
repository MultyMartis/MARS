**Not:** autonomous self-improving AI, runtime governance mutation, universal evolution law, automatic process rewriting, perfect adaptability, or permission to accumulate unlimited rules.

**Core principle:** frontend governance systems must preserve **controlled evolution, continuity-preserving refinement, historical survivability, methodological adaptability, explainable governance change, and governance self-improvement discipline**.

## 1. Positioning

Governance Evolution & Self-Refinement Discipline exists because Website Factory governance now covers organizational memory, adaptive governance, governance prioritization, reasoning transparency, temporal continuity, workflow discipline, provenance governance, context survivability, and implementation survivability.

That coverage is useful only if the governance method itself can improve without losing continuity.

| This layer governs | This layer does not govern |
|--------------------|----------------------------|
| Controlled methodological change, governance renewal, refinement traceability, historical survivability, and continuity-safe evolution | Autonomous self-improvement, runtime policy mutation, hidden governance rewriting, or universal evolution law |
| Human-supervised review of when governance should be preserved, refined, deprecated, split, merged, or clarified | Endless rule accumulation, automatic pruning, or process replacement by model preference |
| Drift vocabulary for stagnation, methodology fossilization, governance ossification, legacy-rule accumulation, and uncontrolled mutation | Redesigning Triumph or any other project |
| Forge reporting discipline for `GOVERNANCE EVOLUTION FINDINGS` | A claim that Website Factory can guarantee permanent adaptability |

The governance question is not "did we add more governance?"  
The governance question is: **can the governance system evolve carefully, preserve its history, explain its refinements, and avoid both fossilization and uncontrolled mutation?**

## 2. Canonical Definition

**Governance evolution** is the disciplined, human-supervised adaptation of governance methods when new evidence, repeated lessons, changed project conditions, or accumulated friction show that the current method needs refinement.

**Self-refinement discipline** is the practice of improving governance without treating the governance system as self-authorizing, self-protecting, or automatically correct.

This layer protects:

- **Controlled evolution** - governance changes are scoped, justified, and reviewable.
- **Continuity-preserving refinement** - refinements preserve historical meaning, source rationale, and prior lessons.
- **Methodological adaptability** - governance can respond to new operational realities without becoming arbitrary.
- **Governance survivability** - methodology remains useful across long projects, handoffs, lessons, and future work.
- **Historical continuity preservation** - old governance context remains readable when rules change, split, merge, or retire.
- **Refinement traceability** - future operators can see what changed, why, what evidence motivated it, and what remains uncertain.
- **Governance renewal** - stale assumptions are reviewed instead of protected by age.

A governance system may preserve continuity, maintain documentation, enforce stability, and appear mature while still becoming obsolete, resisting necessary adaptation, accumulating outdated assumptions, and collapsing through stagnation.

## 4. Core Rules

- **Governance should evolve carefully.** Change is necessary, but method changes need evidence and scope.
- **Continuity matters during evolution.** Refinement that erases history creates a new drift risk.
- **Refinement requires traceability.** A changed rule should explain what it preserves, supersedes, narrows, expands, or retires.
- **Historical survivability matters.** Old rationale should remain readable even when the old rule is updated.
- **Adaptation should remain reviewable.** A future operator should understand why the governance method changed.
- **Frozen methodology becomes fragile.** A process that cannot adapt eventually protects outdated assumptions.
- **Governance change requires proportionality.** Not every friction point warrants a new layer, rule, or permanent checklist.
- **Evolution without continuity is dangerous.** Fast methodological redesign can break trust, lineage, and operational readability.
- **Old process is not automatically correct.** Age can provide evidence, but age alone is not authority.
- **New process is not automatically better.** Novelty can hide uncontrolled mutation, bloat, or broken continuity.

## 5. Governance Evolution Review Questions

Before adding, refining, merging, deprecating, or re-scoping governance, ask:

- What operational evidence shows that the current governance method needs refinement?
- Is the problem stagnation, bloat, ambiguity, under-protection, over-protection, or missing continuity?
- What historical rationale created the existing rule, and is it still valid?
- Does the proposed change preserve source rationale, lessons, authority boundaries, and older report readability?
- Is this a clarification, optional-depth path, deprecation, split, merge, escalation rule, or new governance layer?
- What risk does the refinement reduce, and what new governance mutation risk does it create?
- Can future operators understand what changed without reading private chat history?
- Should the change be documented as reusable methodology, project-specific lesson, experimental pattern, or SAFE UNKNOWN?

## 6. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden drift:

| Anti-pattern | Why it is forbidden |
|--------------|---------------------|
| **Governance fossilization** | Treats old methodology as permanent even when operational evidence changes. |
| **Legacy-rule worship** | Preserves outdated rules because they are familiar or historically respected. |
| **Endless rule accumulation** | Adds rules for every concern without reviewing overlap, usefulness, or proportionality. |
| **Frozen-process absolutism** | Refuses to adapt process even when context, risk, or evidence changes. |
| **Unreviewed governance inheritance** | Carries old assumptions into new work without checking source, scope, or current relevance. |
| **Adaptation resistance** | Treats governance change as weakness rather than controlled renewal. |
| **Uncontrolled methodology mutation** | Changes process silently, locally, or impulsively without traceability. |
| **Continuity-breaking redesign** | Rewrites governance in a way that makes prior reports, lessons, or authority unreadable. |
| **Governance self-protection** | Protects the process from critique even when the process creates friction or drift. |
| **"Old process therefore correct"** | Mistakes survival for validity. |

Use [evolutionary-drift-taxonomy.md](evolutionary-drift-taxonomy.md) for full drift classification.

## 8. Triumph V2 Lessons Captured

Triumph V2 exposed reusable Website Factory governance-evolution lessons:

- V1/V2 contamination showed that governance must evolve while preserving lineage; a new method cannot erase version history.
- Repeated addition of specialist layers created value, but also exposed the need to review whether each layer remains proportional, findable, and non-overlapping.
- Freeze, source, visual, responsive, workflow, context, and memory findings should refine governance only when their lesson is traceable and reusable.
- A successful V2 correction should not become a fossilized universal method; it should remain a scoped lesson subject to future review.
- Old project rules, legacy assets, and previous decisions require renewal checks before becoming current authority.
- Governance maturity is not proven by documentation volume; it is proven by continuity-safe adaptability and readable refinement history.

These are Website Factory governance lessons, not Triumph redesign instructions.

## 9. SAFE UNKNOWN

Record **SAFE UNKNOWN** when:

| Situation | Why it is unknown |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Refinement source is unclear | Cannot prove what evidence, lesson, failure, or drift motivates the governance change. |
| Historical rationale is missing | Cannot tell why the old rule existed or what risk it protected. |
| Governance authority is unclear | Cannot know whether a proposed change is approved methodology, local workaround, or experiment. |
| Continuity impact is unreviewed | Cannot claim refinement preserves old reports, lessons, or authority boundaries. |
| Mutation risk is unknown | Cannot tell whether the method change silently expands, narrows, or redirects governance power. |
| Proportionality is unproven | Cannot know whether the refinement is clarification, optional depth, deprecation, or new bloat. |
| Legacy rule validity is unclear | Cannot decide whether the rule should survive, change, retire, or remain quarantined. |
| Future readability is weak | Cannot prove a future operator can understand what changed and why. |

**Action:** state the missing evolution evidence, identify the resolver, and classify the refinement as preserve, clarify, refine, deprecate, split, merge, experiment, HITL required, blocked, or SAFE UNKNOWN.
